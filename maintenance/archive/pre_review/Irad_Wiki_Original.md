## Article

Irad, also know as Iradistan or The Republic of Irad, is a partially recognized nation of Central Asia in a state of suspended sovereignty. Approximately one third of Irad is within the Irad Special Economic Zone (ISEZ) which is governed by the Democratic Party of Irad (DPI) a remnant of the defunct American territorial government. The remaining two thirds are controlled by native self-governing tribes, nominally aligned with with the Iradi Freedom Society (IAC or Iradin Azatlık Cemiyeti); The territory outside of the ISEZ is collectively known as the Autonomous Tribal Regions. Irad is a disputed territory, it is entirely claimed de jure, but largely not administered by, the neighboring nations of Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Tajikistan, and Kyrgyzstan. Irad has been on the United Nations list of non-self-governing territories since 1992. The population is estimated at about 4.6 million, slightly less than half of which live within the three major cities of the ISEZ: Talas, Nurzhan, and Iyık Janyl.

The modern territory of Irad was created by the Soviet process of national delimitation. In 1925 the culturally and ethnically distinct Iradi people were given a nation of their own for the first time. From 1925 to 1991 the territory was the Iradi Soviet Socialist Republic, also known as Soviet Iradistan, the Iradi SSR, ISSR, or more commonly Iradistan, an union republic of the Soviet Union. It was governed by the Communist Party of Irad, the local branch of the Soviet Communist Party, the sole legal political party at the time. The Communist Party of Irad declared independence on October 9th 1991, preceding the Soviet Union's dissolution on December 26th. 

The newly formed Socialist Republic of Irad implemented controversial policies to quell unrest due to the republic's ongoing economic hardships. Widespread crackdowns on freedom of speech and a reversal of traditional tribal autonomy were met with popular uprisings. After three months of violence the Socialist Party had lost control of Irad's largest city, Talas. On March 11th 1992 the Provisional Government of Irad declared independence and formed the Republic of Irad, triggering full scale civil war. The Provisional Government unanimously voted ratify the Alimbekov-Baker Treaty on June 2nd 1992, and as a result the US military intervened in the conflict with Operation Starlight. By August 18th 1992 all territories of the Socialist Republic of Irad had been annexed by the United States and incorporated into the autonomous territory of Irad. The Republic of Irad became Irad, an organized but unincorporated territory of the US, administered by a territorial legislature and Governor. 

On December 3rd 2006, George W. Bush used executive powers to withdraw the US from the Alimbekov-Baker Treaty, effectively ceding Irad to its own governance. The Supreme Court of Irad ruled that America's withdrawal would trigger Irad's statutory independence clause, forcing the creation of a sovereign state. On December 9th the territorial legislature proclaimed the creation of the Democratic Republic of Irad. International observers have claimed the transfer of power was marred in widespread corruption; US-backed government programs were privatized and transferred to companies under control of Democratic party members. Within weeks violent protests began, and after two months of independence Irad was once again plunged into civil war. On June 4th Talas was stormed by the Popular Front of Irad, tribal leaders reclaimed regional autonomy, and the DRI government was dissolved. 

Following the failure of the DRI, Democratic Party member, Erlan Toktarov, seized control of the Democratic Party of Irad. Toktarov's DPI, commonly called the New Democratic Party of Irad, regained control of urban centers within the ISEZ with the support of the US and western oil companies operating in the region. Toktarov unilaterally reversed the Supreme Court's decision regarding the independence clause and returned Irad to a state of suspended sovereignty. At the time Toktarov's reversal was viewed as a first step towards unification, but public opinion has shifted after more than a decade of stalled reunification negotiations. Irad remains in a state of frozen conflict today.

## Etymology

Yusup Ayyubov's The White Rider Shows the Way features Jesus revealing Irad's star to Janyl
The etymological origin of Irad is disputed. In Iradi there is a distinction between Irad, the name for the star Polaris, and Irad jeri, the land of Irad. In Iradi culture, Irad is not only the physical star, but also the spiritual waypoint that guides man towards Irad, a transcendent place of sublime freedom. Irad jeri refers to the physical land of the Iradi people. In this context Polaris is often referred to as Irad's star instead of Irad to avoid confusion. Iradis are sometimes poetically called the people of the star.

The rich tradition of music and epic poetry in Iradi culture has led some scholars to theorise the etymological origin of the word Irad is related to the Iradi verb "to sing", ırda.

The Iradi Church believes they are the descendants of the biblical figure Irad, who followed his eponymous star to the land of Irad. The Church posits that Irad derives from the Hebrew verb ערד ('rd), to flee, reflecting their belief that the Iradi people are cursed by a form of ancestral sin causing them to search for, but never find a home.

Some modern scholars theorize that the myth of Irad was introduced to Turkic tribes during 11th century by way of the Eastern Syriac Church. The earliest recorded instance of the word Irad in the region was found in the Chronicon an ecclesiastical history written by 13th century West Syrian historian, Gregory Bar Hebraeus.

The Soviet designation of Iradistan is still commonly used in western countries. Iradistan roughly translates to "The place of the people of the star", as -stan is a Persian suffix meaning "place of".

## History

Main article: History of Irad

### Prehistory

The region of Transoxiana has been inhabited since the Upper Paleolithic. The first known settlements in the area correspond to the Kelteminar culture (c. 5500–3500 BCE), a Neolithic society characterized by its fishing, hunting, and rudimentary agricultural practices along rivers and lakes. During the Bronze Age, the region became the southernmost expanse of the Botai culture (3700–3100 BCE), which is the first culture known to have domesticated horses. Archaeological discoveries, including horse bones, bridles, and pottery containing traces of mare's milk, indicate the pivotal role of equine domestication in the Botai's subsistence and mobility.

In the late Bronze Age, the Andronovo culture (2000–900 BCE) migrated into the region, introducing advances in metallurgy, cattle breeding, and seasonal migrations. To the south, the Bactria-Margiana Archaeological Complex (BMAC), also known as the Oxus Civilization, established early urban centers in the area. These cities, situated along fertile river valleys, served as hubs of trade and agriculture. The region of modern Irad was within the shifting marches of Oxus and Andronovo cultures. Over time, some of the nomadic Indo-Iranians adopted the sedentary practices of the Oxus people.

The Sogdians are likely the sedentary descendants of the nomadic Andronovo peoples. The Sogdian city-states emerged as the dominant cultural and economic entity of the region around the 10th century BCE. Sogdia had no central government, though the cities were under the cultural and economic influence of Samarkand. Known for their craftsmanship and trade, Sogdians would later play a pivotal role in the formation of the Silk Road network; their Iranic language became the lingua franca along the Northern Silk Road. This independent Sogdia (900–500 BCE) was contemporaneous with the creation of the Avesta, the holy texts of Zoroastrianism, which spread into the region during this period.

### Early Empires

For nearly two millennia, the cities of Sogdia would pass under the rule of successive empires, beginning with the Achaemenid Empire (500–330 BCE). The region of modern-day Irad was incorporated into Persia under the rule of Cyrus the Great, under the satrapy of Bactria, the empire’s eastern frontier. This era saw the introduction of coin-based currency and connected Transoxiana to the Mediterranean world.

The Hellenistic period began after Alexander the Great's conquest of the Achaemenid Empire in 330 BCE. Transoxiana was the furthest northeastern expansion of Alexander’s empire, becoming synonymous with the furthest extreme of civilization. The region was passed on to the Seleucid Empire and, later, the Greco-Bactrian Kingdom. This era saw the fusion of Greek and local cultures, evidenced by Hellenistic architecture, coinage, and art.

The Saka, Scythian nomads, overran the region around 145 BCE, destabilizing the Greco-Bactrian Kingdom. The Saka were likely driven into Transoxiana by the Yuezhi nomads who had invaded the Saka steppes. By 160 BCE, Chinese records show the Saka ruled over the Greco-Bactrian city-state of Dayuan, located within the Ferghana Valley, renowned for their fast and powerful horses, described by the Chinese as "heavenly horses." Han interest in securing these horses led to the War of the Heavenly Horses in 104 BCE, resulting in Dayuan becoming a Han puppet state, giving China a direct connection to western trade routes for the first time.

The Yuezhi would follow the Saka into Transoxiana and drive the last Greco kings from the region. The Hellenized descendants of the Yuezhi are believed to be the Kushans, who succeeded the Greco-Bactrians. By the 1st century CE, the Kushan Empire rose to dominance, transforming the region into a major hub of trade and cultural exchange. The Northern Silk Road, which connected China to the Mediterranean, was fully developed during this period. Under Kushan rule, the area saw the flourishing of Buddhism, Zoroastrianism, and nascent Christianity, along with artistic and architectural innovations that blended Greco-Bactrian and Indian influences. They were succeeded by the Hephthalites (White Huns), whose rule during the 5th and 6th centuries brought further economic prosperity to the region, as ransoms and plunder were invested into local infrastructure.

### Turkic Rule

The arrival of the Göktürks in the 6th century marked the beginning of Turkic dominance in the region, under the First Turkic Khaganate. Muqan Qaghan, the son of Bumin, the founder of the Khaganate, conquered the Hephthalite Empire, creating the largest empire in the world at the time. The Göktürks adopted the Sogdian language and systems of trade. The Khaganate split into Western and Eastern Khaganates, and by 660 CE, the Western Turkic Khaganate had been completely subjected by Tang China. Modern-day Irad lay within the Sogdian Protectorate of the Chinese Empire.

The 8th century saw the arrival of the Umayyad Caliphate, which brought Islam to the region. The Abbasid Caliphate followed and increased the rate of Islamization in the area, resulting in the decline of traditional religious traditions such as Zoroastrianism and Buddhism and forcing some Sogdian Christians eastwards towards the more religiously tolerant Turkic peoples. During the 10th century, the region came under the control of the Kara-Khanid Khanate, a Turkic dynasty that was the first to convert to Islam.

In the 12th century, the Khanate transformed into the Qara Khitai when it fell under the rule of the Khitan, sinicized nomads known for their tolerance and religious plurality. Under their rule, Nestorian Christianity had a resurgence, coexisting alongside Islam and other faiths. It was also during this period that the first historical mentions of the Iradi people are found. Their origins are debated; some historians suggest they were Keraites, a Nestorian Christian tribe that migrated through the region after their conversion in Merv, while others argue they were remnants of the Naimans, who briefly ruled the Qara Khitai before being displaced by the Mongols.

The Mongol conquests of the 13th century brought the region under the control of the Mongol Empire and later the Chagatai Khanate, one of its successor states. This era saw the region at the center of the vast Mongolian world. During the reign of Ali-Sultan (1339-1342), the Chagatai Khanate saw forced conversion to Islam and repression of other religions.

The rise of the Timurid Empire in the 14th century marked a period of repression for Iradi Christians, who faced widespread persecution under Timur’s rule. Forced to practice their faith in secrecy, the Iradi people struggled to preserve their religious and cultural identity. This hardship continued under the Emirate of Bukhara, where the Iradi population remained marginalized, often excluded from political and social power.

In the 18th century, the Khanate of Kokand emerged as a dominant power in the region. The khanate’s second ruler, Abd al-Rahim Biy, showed appreciation for Iradi fighters by commissioning the construction of a church in 1727. Around this church, the first culturally Iradi city, Iyık Janyl (Saint Janyl), was established. This city became a spiritual center for the Iradi people. Despite their growing influence, the Iradi remained a marginalized faction within the khanate. The Church of Saint Janyl, first described to the west by the British explorer Joseph Wolff, was a symbolic recreation of the original lost Church of Janyl, representing the resilience and devotion of Iradi faith.

### Tsarist Russian Occupation

The end of the 19th century would bring the conquest of the Khanate of Kokand by Tsarist General Mikhail Chernyayev in 1876. This annexation of Kokand’s territories brought the region of modern-day Irad into the new Fergana Oblast of Russian Turkestan. The Russian Empire’s expansion into Central Asia was driven by the Great Game, a global competition for strategic and economic interests. For the Iradi people, this occupation brought significant upheaval, as traditional tribal borders were rewritten and new administrative structures were imposed with the arrival of the first Russian and Ukrainian settlers.

The Semirechye Revolt of 1916, a large-scale uprising across Turkestan, saw Iradi tribal groups resist Russian rule alongside their Muslim neighbors. The revolt was triggered by forced conscription into the Russian army during World War I and escalated into widespread violence against Russian settlers and officals. The revolt ultimately ended with the arrival of the Imperial Russian Army and the violent suppression of the local population. During the final phase of the revolt, some tribes retreated into the eastern mountains during winter and were never seen again. Historians believe these tribes perished due to starvation and extreme winter conditions, but their story has become a favorite subject for Iradi poets and singers. While the Iradi resistance was ultimately crushed, it left a legacy of defiance. In the aftermath, Iradi tribes began organizing in more cohesive forms of intertribal governance.

Enflamed by their failed revolt and inspired by an Iradi language translation of "Catechism of a Revolutionary" by Sergey Nechayev and Mikhail Bakunin, a strain of anarcho-communist ideology took root in tribal Irad during the year leading up to the Russian Civil War. The revolutionary ideals of collectivism and resistance to imperialism resonated deeply with Iradi tribal structures, which historically emphasized communal decision-making and mutual aid. At the outbreak of the war, most Iradi tribesmen sided with the Red Army. It was Iradi irregular horsemen who overthrew the White Army from their seat in Kokand. British forces invaded the region in support of the Whites but the local Reds including Iradis had driven them out by the end of 1918. Their alliance with the Bolsheviks contributed to the establishment of Soviet Iradistan, officially named the Iradi Soviet Socialist Republic (ISSR) in 1925. This was a pivotal moment in Iradi history, marking the first time the region was recognized as a distinct political and administrative entity.

### The ISSR

The Soviet policy of korenizatsiia (nativization) in the 1920s and 1930s sought to cultivate a unique Iradi national identity. For the first time in history the Iradi language was standardized, and local leaders were promoted in the Communist Party to encourage a sense of autonomy within the greater Soviet framework. The city of Nurzhan, originally called Kobozev, was founded as the capital of Iradistan and became the political and economic center of the new Iradi nation. However, the korenizatsiia era was short-lived, as fears of fascist influence on the Catholic Church led to strict policies on state atheism, which reversed the early cultural gains, leading to religious suppression and persecution. By 1937, the policy of nativization was completely abandoned for the new policy of Russification. The Iradi language was suppressed in print, and Soviet schools switched to teaching lessons in Russian only.

The Soviet period also brought significant environmental exploitation, particularly through the forced collectivization of agriculture and the development of kolkhozes, state-run farms. Iradi pastoralists, historically reliant on herding and small-scale farming, were forced into state-controlled agricultural enterprises or had their familial lands taken from them. Tribal Iradis were forced from fertile plains into the mountainous regions of the east. Additionally, the discovery of vast oil and natural gas reserves in Irad during the mid-20th century transformed the region into an economic keystone for the Soviet Union. These oil reserves were developed and heavily exploited, with little benefit to the local population, who remained largely impoverished. 

In the late 1930s, Stalin’s government forcibly relocated tens of thousands of Koryoins, ethnic Koreans from the Soviet Far East, to Central Asia, including the ISSR. The transfer, part of a strategy to secure border regions, caused significant hardship and death. In Irad, many Koreans survived only due to the generosity of Iradis, who kept them from starving by sharing their own meager rations. These Koreans were gradually integrated into the cultural and religious practices of urban Iradis.

The 1956 Talas riots were triggered by the accidental shooting of an Iradi widow’s horse by a Russian soldier at a routine vehicle checkpoint. The incident ignited outrage among the local population, who were already frustrated by perceived economic and cultural marginalization under Soviet rule. Protests quickly escalated into violent clashes between Iradi and Russian residents, with Russian-owned buildings, shops, and vehicles targeted by angry mobs. Soviet authorities responded with force, imposing martial law and arresting over a thousand ethnic Iradis. Hundreds of detained Iradis never returned to the their homes, but were instead deported to distant parts of the Soviet Union.

The Communist Party of Irad declared independence on October 9, 1991, preceding the Soviet Union’s dissolution on December 26. The Socialist Republic of Irad inherited the economic instability, political corruption, and unresolved ethnic and religious tensions of the ISSR, which culminated in the Winter Uprising of 1992. This was a mass protest movement against the ruling elite, who were largely ethnic Russians. The uprising spiraled into violence, and anti-Socialist forces seized the city of Talas. There, the Provisional Government of Irad declared independence from the SRI, forming the Republic of Irad. This ultimately plunged the nascent nation into civil war between the Socialist forces based in Kobozev and the Provisional forces in Talas.

## Geography

Irad is a landlocked territory in Central Asia, bordering Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan and Uzbekistan. As a country the Republic of Irad was one of three doubly landlocked countries in the world - that is, a landlocked country completely surrounded by other landlocked countries. The other two doubly landlocked countries are Uzbekistan and Liechtenstein. All of its rivers flow into closed drainage systems which do not reach the ocean. 

The historic territory of the Iradi people roughly corresponds to the ancient region called Transoxiana (lit. 'Land beyond the Oxus') bound by the Oxus river to the south, the Chu river to the north, the Kyzylkum desert to the west, and the Tien Shan mountain range to the east.

## Politics

Governor Erlan Toktarov shows his prized horse Zharkyn (2018)
Sovereignty over Irad is contested between the Democratic Party of Irad and the Iradi Freedom Society. Neighboring nations of Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Tajikistan, and Kyrgyzstan have claimed the territory and its legal status remains unresolved. The United Nations considers it to be a "non-self-governing territory".

### Toktarov and the DPI

Erlan Toktarov and the DPI claim to be the only legal and democratically elected government of Irad, but only control one third of Irad's territory, mainly within the ISEZ. In November 2011, Toktarov was re-elected as governor for a second term with a margin of 88.74%. During his second term he passed an amendment to reform the 1992 Elective Governor Acts allowing for unlimited successive terms. He has since won three more terms: 2015 with 78.25%, 2019 with 90.31%, and 2023 with 92.75%. Toktarov's son-in-law Aidar Maksat has served as Lieutenant Governor since 2019. The Organization for Security and Co-operation in Europe have stated that the electoral reform and elections that followed lacked "any meaningful transparency or choice for the Iradi people". Althought Toktarov has stated that Irad has had freedom of the press since the fall of the ISSR, the DPI is believed to have complete editorial control over Irad's largest news channel, IradOne.

Leaked US intelligence reports from Toktarov's first term paint him as an autocratic figure, prone to corruption and nepotism. They claimed the DPI was trending toward a cult of personality based around Toktarov who had transformed the ISEZ government into a neopatrimonial regime. They cited Toktarov's elevation of his five sons to positions of political or economic power, and the family's control of local businesses within the ISEZ. The reports concluded that Toktarov has maintained the status quo of Irad's quasi-territorial status in order to avoid conflict with local super powers, specifically Russia and China.

Since July of 2024, Toktarov has not been seen in public, fueling persistent rumors about his deteriorating health. In his absence, Aidar Maksat has taken on an increasingly prominent role, managing the day-to-day operations of the Iradi Special Economic Zone (ISEZ). Meanwhile, Toktarov's two eldest sons, Ruslan and Alikhan, have stepped into the public spotlight more frequently over the past six months, though their increased visibility has not been well-received. Ruslan’s recent inflammatory comments about muslims have done little to dispel his reputation as a racist and brutish figure, with critics questioning his ability to handle complex political or economic matters. Alikhan, on the other hand, has drawn widespread criticism for his irresponsible behavior, with rumors of substance abuse and reckless spending.

### Opposition Within the ISEZ

Kamran Bekzatov is the president of the nation's largest energy company, NurGaz, and is widely considered to be an Iradi oligarch. He belongs to the last generation of urban Iradis who were Russified in Soviet schools during the ISSR. This demographic, often referred to as Russos, is culturally and linguistically aligned with the Russosphere, maintaining strong connections to Russian language, customs, and political ideals. Centered around Nurzhan, the former capital of the ISSR, this generation has capitalized on the remnants of Soviet-era infrastructure to dominate Irad’s industrial sector. Bekzatov is the most prominent and outspoken member of the Russo generation. Recently, he has made headlines for his blunt criticism of the DPI and his overt praise for Vladimir Putin.

Jason Ilyasov is an Iradi-American and self described anarcho-capitalist. Ilyasov has been a regular of IradOne opinion panels. His appearances are characterised by a brash and aggressive demeaner. He has claimed that Irad is in an unique position to become a unregulated corporate paradise and the economic heart of Asia. Originally viewed as a media personality, the support from his fanbase appears to have turned political as recent criticism of the DPI resulted in spontaneous protests in Talas.

### IAC and Tribal Regions

Bektur Zhumabek uulu meeting with eastern tribal leaders (2009)
Iradi Freedom Society or Iradin Azatlık Cemiyeti is a loose confederation of semi-nomadic Iradis who self govern within tribal groups. These tribes control two thirds of Irad's territory, known collectively as the Autonomous Tribal Regions. The Tribal Regions are entirely rural. The Society's unofficial leader is Bektur Zhumabek uulu, who is also the leader of the Tanıp tribe. Bektur has been reluctant to use any titles or language which portray him as a leader of the Society, he has stated his preference to be viewed as an ambassador or negotiator. During the civil war, Bektur was an important figure in the Popular Front of Irad, and is widely viewed as a hero for his actions during the storming of Talas.

### Unification

An UN-sponsored ceasefire agreement between the DPI and IAC has been in place since 2007. A referendum for unification has been proposed since 2008, but no framework for such a unification has been successfully negotiated. According to 2017 polling, the overwhelming majority of Iradis within the ISEZ want a unified sovereign nation of Irad. Polling in Tribal Regions has been limited, but available data also shows support for a unified Irad; there have been numerous claims of DPI interference and bias in referendum polling. In 2011 the only true attempt at bilateral negotiations between the two parties ended with IAC's withdrawal, due to being offered only one third of seats in the legislature. Public statements by tribal leaders have shown broad ambivalence towards Iradi nationhood.

Toktarov's reluctance towards good faith unification negotiations and his continuation of Irad's quasi-territorial status are seen by some as a foreign relations strategy. Irad's Christianity has long put it at odds with bordering muslim nations, and its affiliation with the United States has made it a threat to neighboring foreign powers. The Autonomous Tribal Regions have acted as a buffer for neighboring unrest and border disputes, shielding the ISEZ. Irad's subnational status has allowed Toktarov to deflect conflict with Russia and China towards America. Toktarov has publicly voiced a desire to strengthen relations with China, stating that "Irad's alignment with China is inevitable and welcome".

## Economy

The economy of Irad encompasses significant energy resources, agricultural activities, mining, and contributions from foreign military operations. Despite its potential, the economy faces challenges related to geopolitical tensions and environmental concerns.

### Energy

Oil exploration in Irad dates back to the Soviet era, with established reserves continuing to support the country’s economy. Irad’s current infrastructure only allows it to economically export northwards into Kazakhstan. In the past thirty years there have been several proposed international pipelines, but as of today none of them have materialized.

Irad’s energy sector is dominated by NurGaz (The Nurzhan Oil and Gas Company), the largest oil and gas company in the country, headquartered in Nurzhan. Under the leadership of Kamran Bekzatov, NurGaz oversees the majority of oil and gas production in Irad, most of which is exported north to Kazakhstan and onwards to Russia.

The US annexation of Irad brought several western oil companies into the region. Chevron has been invested in the Iradi energy section since the beginning, but has recently been forced to scale back operations due to the resurgence of the Taliban in Afganistan which has reduced the feasibility of a proposed, US backed, Irad-Tajikistan-Afghanistan-Pakistan pipeline. Previously, Chevron had invested heavily in modernizing existing oil extraction infrastructure and was exploring opportunities for tribal land drilling partnerships. Chevron’s withdrawal has left potential partnerships with other multinational corporations uncertain, though Irad’s government continues to seek foreign investment.

Irad’s natural gas reserves remain underdeveloped due to transportation challenges. However, the China National Petroleum Corporation (CNPC) has proposed a pipeline that would connect Turkmenistan to China via Irad. If completed, this project is expected to accelerate the exploitation of Irad’s natural gas resources.

### Agriculture

Irad is believed to be the geo-genetic birthplace of the domesticated horse, and horse breeding remains a point of pride to Iradis. The small scale and commonly family-run ranches of rural Irad are renown for two main horse types: the Chongtor, a short and sturdy, endurance-based breed suited to mountainous terrain, and the Akyel, a swift and graceful, white or golden breed with historical links to the Ferghana horse. Since the 90s, the United States has been the largest importer of Iradi horses, followed by the United Kingdom and Ireland. Recently, demand has surged in Hong Kong and mainland China.

The agricultural sector also includes beef production, which expanded significantly after the establishment of NAFTA in 1994, during Irad’s period as a U.S. territory. Sukarne, a Mexican multinational corporation, built the largest beef production and processing complex in Irad, located near Talas. This development resulted in a small Mexican community within the city of Talas. 

Conagra also operates several beef processing facilities in the country. Conagra’s investments include introducing advanced supply chain logistics and modernizing meatpacking operations. Conagra brought Iradi ranchers to American to learn how Nebraska’s beef industry operates. Recently, Conagra has also launched partnerships with local farmers and KFC Irad to develop sustainable practices for poultry production, aligning with global trends in protein consumption.

Cotton production is also a key sector in Irad’s economy, although it has caused significant environmental strain due to overuse of water resources. Recurring droughts have further threatened the industry. Recent acquisitions by Xinjiang Lihua Co., a Chinese textile company, have raised concerns over water regulation compliance.

### Mining

Irad’s eastern mountains are believed to hold untapped gold deposits, these deposits exist entirely within the Autonomous Tribal Region. The tribes have allowed mining companies to perform surveys, but the construction of the proposed open pit mines seems like a distant possibility. The country has been a major producer of antimony since the Soviet era, with most of the current output being exported to China.

During its time as a U.S. territory, Irad agreed to refrain from developing its substantial uranium and thorium deposits. These resources remain untouched but represent a potential avenue for future economic growth.

### U.S. Military

The Talas Air Base is the largest U.S. military installation in the region and served as a primary staging area for Operation Enduring Freedom. In addition to Talas AB, the U.S. operates five other bases within Iradi territory, including strategic logistical, testing, and training facilities. These bases play a crucial role in regional security, counterterrorism, and maintaining supply chains for U.S. military operations in Central and South Asia.

The U.S. military presence contributes significantly to the Iradi economy through local employment, infrastructure development, and service contracts. For example, U.S. defense contractors have collaborated with Iradi firms to modernize transportation networks and telecommunications in and around military installations. Irad’s largest ISP, Attelkom, started as a US military contract to modernize the telecom infrastructure around Talas AB. Additionally, U.S. forces have engaged in joint training exercises with the Iradi military, focusing on counterinsurgency and border security, strengthening bilateral ties.

The economic impact of these bases extends to surrounding communities, where local businesses benefit from providing goods and services to military personnel. Despite public criticism of the foreign military presence the strategic and economic benefits have ensured continued cooperation between Irad and the U.S.

## Demographics

Irad's population was estimated at 4,626,000 in 2018. Slightly less than half of Irad's population live in urban centers and the other half in rural areas. 

### Ethnic groups

Irad's largest ethnic group are the Iradis, a Turkic people, who comprise of 72% of the population. The second largest ethnic grouping is consists of neighboring Turkic peoples: 7% Kyrgyz, 5% Uzbeks, and 3% Kazakhs. Other neighboring people include Tajiks, Uyghurs, and Dungans which make up just over 1% of the population combined. The remaining 12% are made up of migrant and foreign worker populations within the ISEZ: 4% Russian, 4% American, 1% Ukrainian, 1% Korean, 1% Chinese, and 1% other, including Mexican. 

Irad is unique in Central Asia for having the largest Mexican population in the region, with over 10,000 in the city of Talas and surrounding area.

### Language

Iradi and English are the official languages of the ISEZ. Russian was dropped as an official language becoming a territory of the US, but remains a de facto lingua franca in the region.

Iradi is a Turkic language of the Kipchak branch, closely related to Kyrgyz, Kazakh, Karakalpak, and Nogay Tatar. It was written in the Arabic alphabet until the twentieth century. Adoption of the Cyrillic script was forced by the USSR in 1941. In 1992 Irad transitioned to use of the Latin script.

### Religion

Irad is unique in its region for being a majority Christian territory. Christianity is the historically dominant religion of the Iradi people. By 2018 estimates 78% of the population is Christian, 18% Muslim, and 4% other groups including Jews, Buddhists, and Baha’is. The Christian population is majority Catholic by way of the Iradi Church, with a minority practicing Russian Orthodoxy, American Evangelicalism, and other forms of Christianity. Iradi Muslims are majority Sunni with a small Shia minority.

The Syro-Iradi Catholic Church, or simply the Iradi Church, is believed to be the last remaining branch of the Nestorian expansion into the Kara-Khitan Khanate. The foundation of the Church and the early organisation of the Iradi people is ascribed to the pseudo-historical figure Janyl during the 11th century. The mythological legends regarding the origin of Irad and it's people are largely Abrahamic in origin.

Although the Iradi Church is unified under the Archbishopric of Iyık Janyl the day to day practices of urban and tribal church members differ greatly. The Church is in communion with the Catholic church as a particular church sui iuris. The Church's distinct liturgical rites are based on local Nestorian traditions, but have experienced rapid liturgical Latinisation since the end of the 20th century. 

Within tribal regions Iradi Christians have practices and beliefs which are distinct, and sometimes heterodox, to their urban counterparts. Scholars have categorized their system of beliefs as an unique folk religion, but most Iradi do not make the distinction. Tribal Christianity is a syncretic form of Nestorianism and Catholicism with elements of folk belief, Animism, Buddhism, and Islam.

Irad has remained secular in all of its incarnations since the adoption of state atheism by the ISSR.

## Culture

### Horse Riding

Horse riding is a deeply ingrained part of Iradi culture, with a variety of traditional equestrian games played across the country. Among the most popular are Ulak Tartysh, a violent team game involving control of a goat carcass, and Oodarysh, a form of horseback wrestling that tests the strength and balance of riders. Kyz Kuu, meaning "chase the girl," is a playful game where a rider attempts to catch another rider while racing, with traditions tied to courtship. Tyin Emmei, or "coin picking," showcases a rider’s agility as they pick a coin from the ground at full gallop. Long-distance horse races, known as At Chabysh, are also a staple of Iradi festivals, often lasting hours and covering vast stretches of terrain. Saddle decorations are a significant form of artistry, with intricate patterns, precious metals, and gemstones used to craft elaborate designs that reflect regional identities, family heritage, and status within the community.

### Falconry

Falconry is a revered tradition in Irad, where hunters train and work with eagles to capture prey. This practice, known locally as burkut saluu, is both a sport and a means of subsistence in rural areas. Hunters, called "berkutchi," maintain a deep bond with their birds, which are often seen as extensions of their own spirit. The training process is rigorous, involving years of dedication to build trust and skill. Competitions and festivals celebrating falconry are common, with displays of speed and precision, and most importantly körktüülük the Iradi concept of effortless grace.

### Music

Irad has an ancient tradition of music and poetry, ranging from acapella style work songs sung on horseback, to epic poems performed with lutes (such as the komuz) and drums. During the Soviet era, Russian ballads gained popularity and influenced local styles, blending traditional melodies with modern arrangements. 

In modern times, Iradi folk music, inspired by American country genres, has developed a unique identity. In the 1980s and 1990s, Irad became a subject of American folk and country songs, which became popular within Irad, leading to the local adoption of these styles. The "Swift Hooves" compilation, featuring American songs about Irad, became a cultural phenomenon in Irad.

The dark electronica genre Mor-Wave originated with the Iradi band tüntolk, whose innovative sound combined haunting production with traditional Iradi lyrics and mythology. Mor-Wave has since inspired subgenres like Mor-Pop and Mor-Folk, with artists often invoking pagan Turkic imagery and themes of nature, transformation, and existentialism. Live performances frequently include bizarre forms of experimental performance art drawing on influences from Soviet era Iradi cinema.

### Film

Iradi Soviet cinema has become known for its experimental and unsettling nature. Director Nurlan Sadikov's Zarina was the first Iradi film shown to a wider Soviet audience. It was meant to be a period action-adventure film, but confused audiences with a non-linear narrative and disturbing visuals. Sadikov’s earlier works, Kok-Terek (The Blue Poplar) and Tolkun (The Wave), were only shown locally and are now considered lost.

The film Zalmax’s Dominion, a Soviet attempt to remake Zardoz, was initially dismissed as incomprehensible by contemporary audiences, but has since become a cult classic for its dadaist style absurdity. The eccentric director Beknur Karimov was a self described “pervert of the screen” and was eventually banned from filmmaking in the Soviet Union due to his unwillingness to remove sexual explicit scenes from his films.

In 1979, two landmark films defined Iradi cinema. Desert Sun’s Rebellion was a tense thriller with a spaghetti Western aesthetic. It received a favorable reception even in America. Walter Goodman wrote for The New York Times that, “this unforgiving pacing ramps up and up with no release … there is a visceral discomfort to it, a feeling that you are there, under the unforgiving sun”, It created the genre of the Iradi Western or Soviet Western and set the tone for the future of Iradi films in the Soviet era. 

The second film of 1979 was Rise of Glory, an ambitious four-hour historical epic tracing Iradi history from the taming of the first horse to the present day ISSR. Audiences were baffled by the film. The first hour shows an Iradi man in a loincloth taming a wild horse in real time with no cuts, the man and the horse often move out of frame. During the jumps between historic periods the films cuts to long expository monologues over a black screen. Most characters and actors are only in the movie for a single scene. The narrator abruptly switches the point of view from first person to second person half way through the film. There were reports of a riot breaking out in a Leningrad theatre showing the film. To this day opinions remain extremely polarized.

Through the 1980s Irad became known as the West of the East and its films largely remained within the broad Iradi Western genre, including Desert Blue, Visions of Irad, Vengeance Rider, Journey of the Steppes, and Guide of the Sands.
