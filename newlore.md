# Irad New Lore and Updates

## Demographic Correction: True Population
* **Wiki Error:** The wiki states the population is 4,626,000 (based on a 2018 estimate).
* **Correction:** The actual population is approximately 6.2 million.
* **Reason for Error:** The 4.6 million figure was sourced directly from the DPI's official state census. Independent demographic researchers and satellite monitoring have since proven that the DPI intentionally suppressed and undercounted the rural population in the Autonomous Tribal Regions to dilute the perceived political influence of the Iradi Freedom Society (IAC).

## Political Geography: The True Seat of Power
* **Wiki Error:** Talas is listed strictly as the political capital of the governorate. 
* **Correction:** While Talas remains the *de jure* capital and the public-facing administrative hub for foreign corporations, the actual *de facto* seat of DPI power has relocated. Governor Erlan Toktarov and his inner circle operate out of "Boz-Korgon" (Grey Fortress), a heavily fortified military-industrial compound 40 kilometers [24.8 miles] east of Nurzhan.
* **Reason for Error:** State media (IradOne) and official territorial documents maintain the legal fiction that Talas is the operational capital to appease Western corporate partners. In reality, rising unrest and security concerns in Talas forced Toktarov to quietly retreat to Boz-Korgon in early 2023.

## The Toktarov Family Dynamics
* **New Lore:** Erlan Toktarov has five sons and one daughter.
* **Ruslan and Alikhan:** The two eldest sons, currently engaged in an increasingly public succession friction.
* **Timur, Adil, and Bekzat:** The three middle sons. They maintain a lower public profile and focus exclusively on managing the family's business endeavors and monopolies within the ISEZ.
* **Zara:** Erlan's youngest child and only daughter. Officially pursuing a doctorate in education from Stanford University.
* **Aidar Maksat:** Erlan's son-in-law (married to Zara) and Lieutenant Governor since 2019. He manages the day-to-day operations of the ISEZ during Toktarov's absence.

## National Vexillology: The Official Flag of Irad
* **Field:** Solid crimson red field, continuing the revolutionary tradition of the 1916 Semirechye uprising and the banner of the ISSR.
* **Central Stallion:** A white horse in gait representing national equestrian heritage, the Chongtor and Akyel breeds, and the historical belief in Irad as the geo-genetic birthplace of the domesticated horse.
* **The Guiding Star:** A five-pointed star positioned directly above the stallion, representing Polaris (*Irad* / *Irad's star*).
* **The Inverted Horseshoe (Fallen Rider Tradition):** Positioned on the hoist side of the stallion. It embodies the steppe cavalry custom of the "Fallen Rider," where downward-facing tack signifies an unmounted warrior who perished in battle. On the national flag, it serves as an official, permanent memorial to the martyrs of Irad—specifically commemorating those lost in the 1916 Semirechye Revolt, Soviet purges, and the civil wars.
* **Arched Inscription:** Inscribed in ancient Orkhon (Old Turkic) script, translating directly to *"Be Free"* in the Iradi language.
* **Wheat Wreath:** Two stylized golden ears of wheat bordering the lower half of the roundel, retaining traditional Soviet-era republican heraldic motifs while symbolizing agricultural life in the river valleys.

## Naming Conventions & De-Russification
* **New Lore:** Starting in the 1990s, Irad underwent a large-scale shift away from Russian-style names. Soviet-era geographical designations are considered obsolete. 
* **City of Nurzhan (formerly Kobozev):** The city of Kobozev was originally named after the real-world Soviet figure Pyotr Kobozev. Following the fall of the ISSR, Amanbek Alimbekov officially renamed the city. During his speech, Alimbekov declared that Nurzhan would be a "guiding light of industry" leading Irad to a new dawn after the ruin of the civil war. "Nurzhan" does not refer to a person; it is a Kipchak-Turkic word translating to "Light of Life." *(Note: Any past apocryphal references to a "Dmitry Kobozev" are strictly non-canon and should be ignored).*
* **Personal Names:** Personal names reflect the deep political and geographic divide. Urban populations within the ISEZ often retain Russian surname suffixes (-ov, -ev, -ova). Conversely, tribal-aligned populations in the rural regions have entirely reverted to traditional Turkic patronymics (-uulu for "son of" and -kyzy for "daughter of").

## Infrastructure & Cold War Intelligence: The Kok-Tash Kariz System
* **New Lore:** Beneath the eastern Tien Shan foothills lies the Kok-Tash Kariz system, an ancient irrigation network expanded during the Kara-Khanid Khanate.
* **Cold War History:** Sections were covertly converted into logistical conduits during the Cold War through CIA-funded operations supporting local Christian and tribal anti-Soviet cells.
* **Public / Journalistic Framing:** In public histories and archaeological surveys, the Kariz system is cataloged as a Silk Road hydraulic marvel. Its use for smuggling by the IAC is reported as an opportunistic exploitation of ancient caves; historical CIA funding remains unacknowledged in official records.

## Religious Figures: Janyl, Mother of Irad
* **New Lore:** The pseudo-historical figure Janyl is revered as the "Mother of Irad." According to tradition, she received a vision of Jesus in the form of the White Rider, who led her to the site of the First Iradi Church (known as the First Church of Janyl). Her historical existence is widely disputed by modern academics, and the actual location of the original church remains unknown.

## Mythology & Superstition: Morkhan and Bovine Omens
* **New Lore:** Morkhan was a pagan god from Iradi pre-Christian beliefs. He is traditionally depicted as a large, dark man with the head of a bull.
* **Christian Syncretism:** Within Iradi Christian tradition, Morkhan became directly associated with Satan.
* **Cultural Impact:** Because of this association, cows and bovines are frequently linked to dark omens or ill fortune in modern Iradi culture, contrasting sharply with their revered view of horses.

## Cultural Addition: Equine Obsession and Dietary Taboos
* **New Lore:** Iradi culture is intensely horse-obsessed, driven by the belief that Irad is the geo-genetic birthplace of the modern domesticated horse. While Iradis regularly consume horse milk products, the consumption of horsemeat is strictly forbidden. Culturally, there is a deep-seated prejudice that views eating horsemeat as a barbarous, uniquely Muslim practice. This taboo carries a stigma roughly equivalent to Western unease surrounding the consumption of dog meat.

## Culinary Traditions and Unique Foods
* **Koryoin Laghman:** A unique variation of the traditional Central Asian laghman noodle dish developed by Korean immigrants in Irad. It is distinguished by the addition of sesame and kimchi, fusing local staples with Korean flavors.
* **Jasil Drink:** A unique Iradi beverage dating back to the Soviet era, made with a vibrant green herb syrup. Its primary flavor profile is tarragon, but it contains various other herbs depending on the regional or household version.
* **Beet Suzma:** A traditional Iradi variation of suzma (strained yogurt/curd) that incorporates beets, giving the dairy product a distinct flavor and striking color.
* **Iradi Plov:** A regional variation distinct from standard Central Asian plov due to its absolute avoidance of horsemeat (utilizing prime grain-finished beef or high-altitude mountain mutton instead, with any *kazy* made of beef or lamb). It uses cold-tolerant, short-grain sticky rice introduced to the lower Chu River Basin by Koryo-saram deportees, is seasoned with Wild Mountain Cumin from the Talas Alatau, and is traditionally served with Beet Suzma to offset the meat's richness.

## Linguistic and Generational Shifts
* **New Lore:** Irad has experienced a profound linguistic and cultural shift since the 1980s.
    * **Urban Boomers & Gen X ("Russos"):** Raised in a Soviet-dominated media landscape, this demographic identifies as "Russos." They remain culturally and linguistically tethered to the Russosphere, maintain Russian customs, and largely hold right-leaning political views.
    * **Urban Millennials:** Raised in Russian-speaking households but forced to learn English post-ISSR. Due to underfunded educational infrastructure and a lack of qualified teachers, their English proficiency carries a distinct, rigid ESL quality.
    * **Zoomers:** The first generation to achieve broad, systemic English fluency. They are also driving a massive cultural resurgence of the Iradi language, mirroring the revival of Gaelic in Ireland.
    * **Tribal Regions:** Rural, tribal-aligned populations actively reject both Russian and English, having continuously maintained Iradi as their exclusive language.

## International Cultural Impact: Iradimania
* **New Lore:** "Iradimania" was a brief but intense period of American cultural fascination with Irad lasting from approximately 1992 to 1994. Following the collapse of the Soviet Union and the establishment of US territorial administration, the American public became captivated by the perceived cultural parallels with Iradi society, romanticizing them as "freedom-loving Christian cowboys" of the Central Asian steppe.
* **The "Swift Hooves" Compilation (1993):** The high-water mark of Iradimania was the 1993 release of *Swift Hooves*, a highly successful compilation album featuring country and folk songs about Irad written and performed by prominent American artists. All profits from the album were directed towards Christian-run charities operating within Irad.

## Geopolitical Development: 2025 Trump Remarks and Backlash
* **New Lore:** In 2025, Donald Trump publicly stated that he was exploring selling Irad to Russia to keep China from acquiring it. It remains unknown whether he realized that Irad had ceased to be an unincorporated U.S. territory decades earlier after the withdrawal from the Alimbekov-Baker Treaty in 2006. The remark provoked intense outrage across the country, triggering widespread demonstrations and fueling acute anti-American sentiment within both the ISEZ and the Autonomous Tribal Regions.

## Cultural Institutions
* **Irad Palace of Art:** The nation's foremost gallery and art preservation foundation. It is headquartered in the city of Iyık Janyl.
* **Irad Film Society:** A non-profit organization dedicated to the preservation and restoration of Iradi films. They organize the biannual Talas International Film Festival. The festival historically features a large slate of Russian-language films. While 2005 is widely considered its most successful year, attendance and participation have fallen off in recent years.
* **Nurzhan Polytechnic Institute:** Formerly known as the Kobozev Polytechnic Institute, it is the nation's foremost technical university, specializing in Petroleum Engineering.

## Major Domestic Corporate Entities
1. **NurGaz (The Nurzhan Oil and Gas Company):** Founded as an ISSR state enterprise in Nurzhan, privatized after 1991 into an oligarchic firm led by Kamran Bekzatov. Controls domestic hydrocarbon extraction, piping crude north toward Kazakhstan and Russia.
2. **Attelkom:** Formed during the US territorial period from a defense telecommunications contract servicing Talas Air Base. Largest domestic ISP and telecom provider in the ISEZ.
3. **IradOne:** Founded in the early 1990s as the state public broadcaster, consolidated under direct DPI editorial control. Programming is primarily in English with some Iradi language content. The company also operates a Russian-language sister channel, **ИрадОдин (IradOdin)**, which is widely considered to have significantly lower production values and worse quality programming compared to the main channel.
4. **Talasbank (Bank of Talas):** Chartered in 1993 following financial reforms and dollarization under the territorial administration.
5. **Sary-Bulak Antimony Works (SBAW):** Heavy metallurgy firm established in 1961 during Soviet industrialization; privatized in 1994, exporting antimony ores to China.
6. **Akyel Equine Logistics & Breeding Syndicate:** Manages pedigree certification and global transport logistics for Chongtor and Akyel horses.
7. **Trans-Chuy Freight & Logistics:** Commercial logistics carrier running bonded trucking routes across the ISEZ and northern border crossings.
8. **Zhetysu Industrial Textiles:** Light-manufacturing plant producing wool, felt, and cotton thread, facing water shortage bottlenecks.
9. **Iradiya (Irad Air Services):** Regional charter and air-cargo operator founded in 1997 operating out of civil hangars adjacent to Talas Air Base.
10. **Nurzhan Agro-Industrial (NAI):** Consolidated agricultural firm specializing in animal feed milling, silage distribution, and bulk grain supply.

## Territorial Footprint & Borders
* **New Lore:** Irad's territory largely overlaps the Talas and Jalal-Abad regions of Kyrgyzstan, together with the northern and eastern rim of the Fergana Valley in Uzbekistan (rural and foothill districts only; the valley's large cities, such as Namangan and Andijan, lie outside Irad) and parts of the Turkistan and Jambyl regions of Kazakhstan.
* **Neighbors and Claimants:** Irad borders only Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, and Kyrgyzstan, and all three claim it *de jure*. Tajikistan neither borders Irad nor claims it. Irad has no border with China or Afghanistan, and remains doubly landlocked.
* **Corrects:** Earlier references to four neighboring claimant states, a Tajik border, and an "Afghan border" are superseded. Tajiks remain a small minority population (about 1% with Uyghurs and Dungans). The antimony sites formerly named after Sary-Tash and Kadamjay (real places in Kyrgyzstan's Osh and Batken regions, outside Irad) are renamed **Sary-Bulak** (mine) and **Chatkal** (smelting and processing), matching their placement in the southern Chatkal and Talas Alatau foothills.

## Real-World Features Within the Footprint
* **Toktogul Reservoir and the Naryn Cascade:** The Soviet-built Toktogul Hydroelectric Station and its reservoir on the Naryn River lie in Irad's Jalal-Abad territory. The station's release schedule balances winter power generation against summer irrigation demand downstream, which makes it a recurring point of friction with Uzbekistan and Kazakhstan.
* **Mailuu-Suu Uranium Legacy:** The valley town of Mailuu-Suu processed uranium ore for the Soviet atomic program from the 1940s into the late 1960s, leaving tailings dumps in landslide-prone terrain. This is the known legacy behind the territorial-era pledge not to develop Irad's uranium and thorium deposits.
* **Arslanbob and Sary-Chelek:** The Arslanbob walnut forests, regarded as among the largest natural walnut forests in the world, and the Sary-Chelek Biosphere Reserve are well-known natural sites in the Chatkal range. Walnuts and wild fruit are a rural export in the tribal regions.
* **Battle of Talas (751):** The clash between Abbasid and Tang forces in the Talas River valley is part of Irad's regional history. Tradition links it to the westward spread of papermaking, though historians dispute that.
* **Fergana Rim:** The northern and eastern Fergana Valley holds the irrigated cotton and orchard belt behind Irad's cotton sector and its water-compliance concerns. It also ties to the Dayuan "heavenly horses" and the Akyel breed's links to the Fergana horse already described in the baselore.

## The Toktogul Complex and the Naryn Water and Power Protocol
* **Split Control:** The Toktogul dam, reservoir, and generating hall lie on the tribal side of the Qara-Chik Demarcation Line, in the Autonomous Tribal Regions. The complex's high-voltage switchyard, the national dispatch centre, the downstream stations of the Naryn cascade, and every export transmission line lie in ISEZ-administered territory. Neither side can release a cubic metre of water or sell a megawatt of power without the other.
* **Output:** Toktogul was built with 1,200 MW of capacity, which makes Irad a net electricity exporter, mainly to Uzbekistan and Kazakhstan, alongside its oil sector.
* **The Protocol:** The 2007 Bishkek Ceasefire Agreement includes the Naryn Water and Power Protocol, which sets the annual release schedule between winter generation and summer irrigation. A joint technical commission of ISEZ and ATR engineers runs it under UN supervision, with observers from Uzbekistan, Kazakhstan, and Kyrgyzstan. This is the main reason all three neighbors sit at the ceasefire table.
* **Public / Journalistic Framing:** Officially, the arrangement is a technical cooperation agreement and revenue is shared under a formula that has never been published. Both the DPI and tribal leaders deny profiting at the other's expense, and independent analysts dispute how the revenue is actually divided. Either side's walkout would risk the ceasefire, which both treat as an unspoken reason to keep the status quo.

## Canon Decisions from the 2026 Wiki Review
These decisions were settled during the full review of the three wiki volumes. They supersede conflicting wording in `baselore.md` and `Irad_Wiki_Original.md` until those files are revised. Full detail and sources are in `CHANGELOG_wiki_review.md`.

* **Timeline clarification:** Toktarov proclaimed the ISEZ from Nurzhan on **25 February 2007**, which began the Second Civil War. The Popular Front stormed Talas on **4 June 2007**, dissolving the Democratic Republic of Irad, and the DPI took the cities that summer. The September 2007 Bishkek Ceasefire froze the line. (This refines the single-sentence wording in baselore's ISEZ entry.)
* **Legal status:** Western Sahara model. Irad has been on the UN list of non-self-governing territories since 1992 and stays listed "by US demand" because Washington never notified the UN that its administration ended in December 2006; the US neither reports on the territory nor seeks its removal. Twenty-six states recognised the Democratic Republic of Irad; a minority keep some recognition of a "Republic of Irad". The Supreme Court's **Statutory Independence Ruling** (Chief Justice Dastan Isakov) is dated **7 December 2006**. Territorial-era residents were **US nationals, not citizens**; that status ended in December 2006.
* **Administrative divisions:** ISEZ = three cities (Talas, Nurzhan, Iyık Janyl) and five districts (Talas, Kara-Buura, Chu, Sary-Bulak, Lower Naryn; Lower Naryn's centre is Tash-Kömür, also the UNMI sector HQ). ATR = 24 clan territories with no formal districts; the UN uses six planning zones (Northern Steppe, Karatau, Talas Alatau, Chatkal Highlands, Toktogul–Naryn, Fergana Rim). The Qara-Chik line is 420 km. Toktogul town, Kara-Köl and Mailuu-Suu lie on the tribal side.
* **Geography composition:** most of Irad's 196,754 km² is steppe and desert rangeland in the north (the lower Chu lowlands, the Moiynkum sands **between the Chu and the Talas**, the southern Betpak-Dala **north of the Chu**, and the Karatau foothills), elsewhere part of Kazakhstan's Jambyl and Turkistan regions. Most people live in the river valleys. The Talas ends in the Moiynkum; the Chu ends near the Akzhaykyn depression on the Northern Steppe.
* **Population arithmetic:** the 2018 city figures cover all territory under each city government; metro and agglomeration figures are estimates outside the census. Only about 125,000 ISEZ residents live outside the three cities, so ISEZ villages are small and district totals are never given. The 1989 Soviet census counted 4,257,000.
* **Legislature and governorship:** territorial era: an interim legislature from October 1992 (bicameral: Senate 15, House 35), first general elections 1994. Since 2007: a single-chamber *Mejlis* of 60 seats (2023: DPI 51, independents 6, Russo-aligned 3); Speaker since 2019 **Nurbek Sultanov** (DPI). The **Elective Governor Acts** are territorial statutes of December 1992: four-year terms, governor and lieutenant governor elected on a joint ticket on the first Tuesday after the first Monday in November, with terms starting on the fourth Monday of November; the two-consecutive-term limit was removed in 2013. Legal scholars differ on whether a formal declaration of incapacity is needed before the Lieutenant Governor exercises the Governor's powers in his own right; none has been made. Maksat was Toktarov's running mate in 2019 and 2023 (Daniyar Tologonov in 2011 and 2015). Territorial governors: Ermatov (1995–2003), Abdrakhmanova (2003–06); US High Commissioners: Ashby, Whitaker, Lindqvist, Garrity.
* **First Civil War:** the siege of Talas ran **10 April – 18 June 1992**, broken by Operation Starlight (began 6 June 1992); about 14,000 dead in the war.
* **2018 border clashes:** with **Uzbek** border guards, June 2018, on the western desert rangelands. The **Northern Border Demarcation Protocol** (2008) is not a treaty: it is UN-facilitated technical minutes between the ISEZ and Kazakh border and customs services, which Kazakhstan says do not affect its claim; patrols are coordinated, not joint.
* **UNESCO:** the Church of St. Janyl and the Ruins of Old Iyık Janyl were inscribed together as one property in **2004** on a US nomination; the Kok-Tash Kariz and Rabat-i-Safed were added in **2014** as Kazakh and Kyrgyz components of the Silk Roads listing. Irad itself is not a State Party. (This corrects the "four sites" framing above.)
* **Church and calendar:** the Feast of St. Janyl is **24 August** (pilgrimage season late August–September). Church tradition places Janyl in the **11th century**; some chronicles associate her with Qara Khitai rule in the 12th. The church at Iyık Janyl was founded in 1727 and consecrated in 1730. Kokand built the Talas citadel fort in the **1820s**. The Cathedral Seminary of Iyık Janyl is formally St. Ephrem Major Seminary. The church uses the Gregorian calendar.
* **Economy:** Irad has **one refinery**, Nurzhan Refinery No. 1 (late 1950s, expanded 1974, 50,000 bpd nameplate). Crude leaves through the 85-km **Northern Export Feeder** into the KazTransOil network near Shymkent (opened November 2000), never through the CPC. The **som** has been fixed at **5 per US dollar** since 1993 (100 tyiyn), issued by the **Bank of Irad** (1993 currency act; reconstituted 2008) under currency-board rules alongside the dollar.
* **Symbols and holidays:** the flag was adopted by decree in May 1992 and confirmed by the State Symbols Act 1993. The anthem *The Spirit of Irad* is by Asan Jumabaev (music) and Cholpon Myrzakulova (words). ISEZ public holidays are set by the Holidays and Commemorative Days Act (1993, revised 2008). The ulak tartysh trophy is the **President's Cup** (instituted 1992 by Alimbekov). The Talas film festival is **biennial** (odd-numbered years), not "biannual".
* **State Archives fond scheme:** 3 Kokand and church charters; 21 Northern Semirechye and Talas districts (1916–36); 85 Central Committee of the Communist Party; 86 Talas City Committee; 108 Revolution of 1992 and Provisional Government; 110 territorial legislative journals; 111 Supreme Court reports; 131 military records; 160 hydrological surveys; 175 ethnography; 204 Iradneft; 236 collective farms; 501 ISEZ executive directives.

## Iyık Janyl Relocated to the Fergana Foothills (Lore Change, October 2026)
* **New Lore:** Iyık Janyl lies at 1,280 m in a side valley in the foothills of the Fergana Range, on the east side of the Naryn gorge about 20 km north-east of Tash-Kömür. It sits inside the ISEZ's southern salient on the Naryn and is surrounded by the Lower Naryn District, while remaining a city of governorate significance administered separately from the district. It is reached from Talas by the Fergana Mountain Transit Highway over the Fergana Gateway and a 12-km branch road above Tash-Kömür, about 235 km in all, so the holy city depends on the same guarded corridor as the Naryn power stations.
* **Climate:** dry-summer continental (Köppen *Dsa*): July highs about 31 °C, January lows about −7 °C, about 560 mm of precipitation, mostly in spring.
* **Setting:** the Qara-Chik line runs along the crests north and east of the city (Toktogul–Naryn zone of the ATR), and the Mailuu-Suu valley of the Fergana Rim lies to the south. The main minorities are Kyrgyz, Uzbeks and Russians. The archeparchy covers the lower Naryn and the Fergana foothills.
* **Corrects:** earlier text placing the city in a side valley on the northern slopes of the Talas Alatau, 65 km south-east of Talas. The legend of Janyl's cave in the Talas Alatau and the Kokand-era history (including incorporation into the Fergana Oblast in 1876) are unchanged; the Fergana-side location fits them.

## Name of the Republican Gendarmerie (October 2026)
* **Origin:** *jandarm* / *jandarmeriya* is a **Russian loanword** (Russian *zhandarm*, ultimately from French), inherited from the tsarist Separate Corps of Gendarmes (1827–1917), which served in Turkestan. It is not a direct French or Turkish borrowing; Irad has no French ties and the force was not modeled on the Turkish Jandarma.
* **Choice of name:** the Provisional Government adopted it in 1992 to distinguish the new force from the Soviet *militsiya* and internal troops.
* **Connotation:** the word's tsarist political-police associations make *jandarm* a term of abuse in opposition and exile writing, sometimes linked to the repression after 1916. Wiki: Vol2 4.52, "Name".

## Former Opposition Parties: Jer-Suu and Labor (October 2026)
* **Jer-Suu Party** ("Land and Water"): Iradi nationalist party, founded Talas 1995 by poet-philologist Tilek Sharshenov and rural notable Kubanychbek Toktobolot uulu. Demanded repudiation of the Alimbekov-Baker Treaty, closure of US bases, pasture/water protection against mining leases, Iradi as sole state language; opposed the dollar-linked som. 2–4 of 35 House seats, 1996–2006 (Talas, Talas valley). Sat in the DRI assembly; walked out over Orozbekov's pasture decrees (Feb 2007); many members joined the Popular Front. **Banned April 2008** by the Supreme Court under the Extremist Activity Act (2008) for taking part in armed rebellion. Sharshenov in exile in Bishkek; Toktobolot uulu and others joined the IAC Chancellery. Wiki: Vol2 4.146.
* **Labor Party of Irad** (*Irad Emgek Partiyası*): social-democratic successor to the Communist Party of Irad, founded Nurzhan March 1993 by railway engineer Gennady Shevchuk and refinery/rail/machine-plant unions; base = Russian/Ukrainian-speaking industrial workforce. Ran Nurzhan city council 1993–2007; 5–7 House seats, 1–2 Senate. **Backed Toktarov in Feb 2007** (council lent him the municipal police). **Made powerless:** failed to re-register under the **Political Parties Act 2010** (10,000 members, at least 500 in each of the eight constituencies) and was dissolved May 2011. Its deputies now sit as Russo-aligned independents on NurGaz-funded civic lists; the name survives in the Labor Association charity. Elena Petrova was first elected on its list (1997). Wiki: Vol2 4.147.
* **Since 2011** no party other than the DPI has met the 2010 Act's thresholds.

## Iradi Christianity: Structure, Symbolism and the Malka (October 2026)
* **Design note (not in-world):** Iradi folk Christianity is modeled structurally on Altai Burkhanism (the 1904 "White Faith") layered over the Church of the East. Burkhanism is a structural parallel only; there is no in-world Burkhanist influence or 20th-century "White Faith" movement in Irad.
* **White against black:** white (the White Rider's horse, milk, felt) marks the holy: white ribbons on crosses and shrine trees, the white *elechek* headdress of married women. Black is Morkhan's color; cattle carry bad omens.
* **No blood sacrifice:** offerings are milk, kymyz and juniper (*arsha*) smoke. Animal sacrifice is rejected as pagan or Muslim; Iradis contrast their milk offerings with Kurban Ait.
* **Shamans:** *bakshy* are regarded as Morkhan's servants, condemned since at least the Kokand period; herbal healers (*tabyp*) are tolerated.
* **Scripture:** Revelation 19:11 (the rider on the white horse) is the defining devotional text; folk belief identifies him with Janyl's White Rider and imagines the Second Coming as a horseman's return.
* **Images:** older rural churches center on a bare cross without a corpus (East Syriac style), often rising from a lotus or cloud like the medieval Chu valley gravestones. Icons are mostly of St. Janyl. Janyl fills much of the Marian role in rural piety; post-1992 Marian statues are seen as a Latin import.
* **Islam:** shared customs (shrine pilgrimage, *bata*, same-day burial, 40th-day memorial) coexist with identity-marking refusals: horsemeat, animal sacrifice, male circumcision, Arabic religious vocabulary (Syriac/Sogdian used instead; Persian-derived words like *Kudai* tolerated).
* **Malka (holy leaven):** survives as a tribal tradition in the ATR (mainly Chatkal valley and eastern mountains). Called *ene achytky* ("mother leaven") by herders; said to descend from Janyl's bread at the First Church; kept in a felt-wrapped wooden pot, renewed each Holy Thursday, carried between pastures; used for leavened Qurbana in yurt liturgies instead of kümöch. Given, never sold; given to new priests and carried by brides from their mothers' households; losing it is a bad omen. The archeparchy requires unleavened kümöch in parishes (a 1990s Latinization); the Synod tolerates the malka where it is ancient custom, and IAC clans treat it as a mark of independence. Wiki: Vol2 4.16 (Kümöch), Vol1 Religion in Irad.

## The Iradi Cross (October 2026)
* **Canon cross:** the *salyba* (from Syriac *ṣlibā*) is the Iradi cross and emblem of the Syro-Iradi Catholic Church (adopted by the Synod in 1994). Equal-armed, concave-sided and flared; three beads (*monchok*) at the end of the upper and side arms; a small ring at the crossing (the empty tomb); no corpus; the shorter lower arm rises from a five-petal lotus (sometimes drawn as a cloud). White on crimson in textiles; low relief in stone and wood. Variant with the eight-pointed star of Irad at the crossing on parish seals and the archeparchy's arms. Image: `iradi_cross.svg`.
* **Origin:** the 13th–14th-century *kayrak* gravestones of the Chu valley and Old Iyık Janyl. Survived in yurt woodwork, bread stamps and felt (Chatkal *shyrdak* makers called it "the flower"); revived in the 1990s against imported Latin crosses and crucifixes.
* **Readings:** church: beads = Trinity, lotus = purity of Christ. Folk: the lotus is "the cloud" of Christ's return (Rev. 1:7), linked to the White Rider; also "Janyl's flower". Worn as silver pendants; emblem of the Sisters of the Cross of Irad. Wiki: Vol2 4.148.

## Yusup Ayyubov the Painter; Actor Renamed (October 2026)
* **Correction:** **Yusup Ayyubov** is a painter, not an actor (as in the original wiki, where his painting *The White Rider Shows the Way* shows Jesus revealing Irad's star to Janyl). The actor of *Desert Sun's Rebellion* (1979), *Zarina* (1968) and *Rise of Glory* (1979), 1938–2019, is renamed **Almaz Sarybaev** (Vol2 4.3).
* **The painter (1869–1937; Iradi *Yusup Ayyub uulu*):** life loosely modeled on the Altai painter Grigory Choros-Gurkin, set in Irad's history. Christian herding family, upper Talas valley; trained in the icon workshop of the Church of St. Janyl; auditing student at the Imperial Academy of Arts, St. Petersburg, 1897–1903 (took the surname Ayyubov there). House-studio at Besh-Tash; Talas Alatau landscapes in an icon-like light; exhibited Tashkent 1907, Verny 1910. *The White Rider Shows the Way* (1913) given to the cathedral for the Feast of St. Janyl: night hillside track; the White Rider (white robe and hood, white horse) points to a single blazing star; Janyl on a dark horse further down the path; a plain below scattered with the lights of the clans' fires; image `The_White_Rider_Shows_the_Way.jpg` on the wiki. Sheltered refugees in 1916 and drew the flight over the passes. 1917: chaired the schools and culture commission of the Iradi National Council (Talas; clergy, teachers, townspeople; autonomy within a Russian federation), dissolved by the Tashkent Soviet in 1918. Exile in Ghulja, Xinjiang, 1920–1925; returned under korenizatsiia; taught at Talas's first art studio, illustrated Latin-script schoolbooks, privately painted for the church (banners and vestments for Mar Timotheos Alimbekov). Arrested October 1937 (nationalist organization, espionage for the Vatican), shot in Talas 14 November 1937; burial place unknown. Rehabilitated 1957; religious works hidden until 1992; now the national painter. House-museum at Besh-Tash; main collection at the Irad Palace of Art. Wiki: Vol2 4.149.

## Virgin Lands Campaign, Beef and Field Maize (October 2026)
* **New lore:** the Virgin Lands campaign targeted the Iradi SSR (from 1954; ~450,000 ha plowed north of the Chu and in the lower Talas, ~30 sovkhozes) and was **relatively successful** compared with other areas: near irrigation, an early switch (after 1957) from continuous spring wheat to wheat–maize–alfalfa rotation with shelterbelts, and a turn to cattle; it avoided the 1960–65 dust-storm collapse of northern Kazakhstan.
* **Origin of the Iradi beef industry:** virgin-land sovkhozes specialized in beef by the early 1960s (Kazakh Whiteheaded crossed with Talas-valley Aulie-Ata cattle); 1970s state fattening complexes near Talas and in the lower Chu. Beef went to the all-Union fund, so mutton stayed the main festive meat until the 1990s. Sovkhoz cattle work was done mainly by Slavic and Korean settlers ("cattle are for the settlers"), which let the Morkhan cattle omens survive among Iradis. SuKarne (1999) was built on a former fattening complex; the territorial period made the industry an export one.
* **Field maize:** introduced as feed under Khrushchev's maize drive (from 1955; breeding station near Kara-Bulak, 1956); succeeded in Irad's hot irrigated valleys. Irad still grows yellow dent field maize, mainly for compound feed (NAI) and the feedlots. Street food: boiled cobs (*jügörü*). Semiz tortillerías use local yellow dent ("Talas tortillas" are yellow).
* **Supersedes:** earlier text calling commercial beef simply "a legacy of the territorial period". Wiki: Vol2 4.150.

## Semiz: the Mexican District of Talas (October 2026)
* **Renaming:** the Mexican enclave of Talas is **Semiz**, not "Nuevo Talas" (retired). It grew from *Otkormochny*, the Soviet workers' settlement of a state cattle-fattening complex (Virgin Lands era) in the western corridor, renamed *Semiz* ("fattened, plump", an Iradi calque) in the 1990s de-Russification. SuKarne (1999) stands on the fattening complex's land.
* **Names in use:** official *Semiz*; Russos still say *Otkormochny*; Mexican residents say *la Colonia*; Iradis colloquially say *Meksikanka* (Russian-style nickname, like Soviet "Nakhalovka"/"Shanghai" districts).

## ATR Clan Politics and the Kurultai (October 2026)
* **Groupings:** commentators speak informally of a "north wing" (*tündük kanat*) and a "south wing" (*tüshtük kanat*), terms with no formal standing. North: the mountain clans of the Talas Alatau (Tanıp, Karakoyun, Akjele), core of the 2007 Front and the mining moratorium, plus the steppe clans of the Northern Steppe and Karatau, led by the Boztorgoy, who depend most on cross-line trade. South: Naiman and Sarybagysh (Chatkal), Kökjal (Toktogul basin), Chubar (Fergana Rim); more ethnically mixed, focused on water, Toktogul and trade with Uzbekistan.
* **Karakoyun seniority:** the sanjyra names the Karakoyun founder as the eldest son of the clans' common ancestor, so the Karakoyun hold the senior seat (*tör*) at the Kurultai, though the Tanıp are more prominent.
* **Akjele** ("white tether line"): mountain clan of the upper Besh-Tash valley; the Tanıp's closest ally; Bektur's mother Zuura Tashtan kyzy was Akjele.
* **Boztorgoy** ("grey lark"): leading steppe clan; Moiynkum winter pastures; grazes along the northern sector facing Nurzhan; keeps more cattle than the mountain clans (mocked as the "cattle clan") and sells feeder cattle and lambs to the Chu plain feedlots and feed mills. *Biy* since 2012: **Sabyrbek Duishön uulu** (b. 1966), commander of the Front's steppe detachments in 2007. Main rival of the Tanıp; seen in ISEZ business circles as pragmatic; publicly supports the IAC and moratorium; elders have said they would consider Muyunkum gas surveys on royalty terms.
* **Kökjal** ("grey mane", a wolf epithet): Toktogul basin clan, many families resettled by the reservoir in the 1970s; elder **Tabaldy Sultanaly uulu** (b. 1949) of Ozgörüsh speaks for the flooded-pasture claims.
* **Chubar** ("dappled"): main Iradi clan of the Fergana Rim around Bazar-Korgon and the lower Kara-Unkur; about a third of households Muslim (Chancellery estimate) through intermarriage with Uzbeks and Kyrgyz; *biy* **Döölötbek Ashym uulu** (b. 1957), a Christian, with Muslim elders on the council. Some northern elders object to Muslim aksakals joining the Kurultai's opening prayers. Chubar elders joined the condemnation of Ruslan Toktarov's December 2024 remarks; the August 2025 Kurultai said the clans "know no guests among their own".
* **Trade permits:** clan councils issue the travel permits that the Gendarmerie registers; the Chancellery divides the seasonal bulk contracts. Until 2019 the Boztorgoy held what other clans saw as an outsized share. **August 2019 quota roll:** contract volumes are now shared by herd numbers reported to the Chancellery; the Boztorgoy stood aside.
* **Water split:** Kökjal demand flooded-pasture compensation before drawdown; the Chubar and other Fergana Rim clans want summer releases and side with the Uzbek observers on that point.
* **Pasture:** recurring cases include Boztorgoy autumn corridors across Karakoyun and Tanıp land to the desert margin, and crowding of camps on the Aksay Plateau.
* **Succession (observers' views only):** Bektur is 58 (b. 15 Aug 1968), with no rule and no public comment from him. Tanıp *biy*: acclaimed by a clan assembly; often named is his eldest son **Nurbolot Bektur uulu** (b. 1990), captain of the Tanıp El-Choro detachment since 2017; elders say standing outweighs descent. IAC spokesmanship: personal, not an office. Sabyrbek is named as the steppe candidate; southern elders float rotation or sharing; others expect the Chancellery to provide continuity.
* **Kurultai procedure:** the second or third week of August, in a ring of white yurts at the Aksay Plateau grounds; inner assembly ~250–300 (biy or senior aksakal, elders, pasture captain and *choro bashı* per clan; the seven tribunal judges; Bektur), with several thousand herders camped around. Seating by sanjyra seniority, Karakoyun at the *tör*; the Chancellery keeps the seating roll. Extraordinary sessions (*kezeksiz kurultai*) meet in winter at the Kara-Suu Tribal Elders Council Hall or the Kara-Koyun Valley.
* **Ritual:** opening *bata* by the eldest aksakal present (Biybolot Moldosh uulu since 2015), then an Iradi prayer by a Syro-Iradi priest and juniper smoke; kymyz shared; no animal killed. Decisions are sealed with a bata and entered in the Kurultai ledger (*kurultai depteri*); couriers carry them to absent clans.
* **Speaking and consensus:** the secretary puts the business; clans speak in reverse seniority (most junior first, Karakoyun last); the presiding Karakoyun elder states the "sense of the assembly" and Bektur reports it outside. A decision passes when no *biy* objects. Objection = laying one's whip (*kamchy*) on the felt (blocks). Alternative = "standing aside" (*chetke turuu*): the decision passes, but the clan is excused from terms concerning its own land.
* **Women:** not delegates; since 2021 a women's council meeting alongside may send written petitions that the secretary reads out.
* **Chancellery secretary (*katchı*):** **Kubanychbek Toktobolot uulu** (Jer-Suu co-founder) since 2016; no vote, does not speak to substance.
* **Notable sessions:** 2007 (constituted the IAC, El-Choro, Chancellery, tribunal); 2011 (approved the Kara-Oi walkout); 2017 (refused DPI census enumerators); 2019 (quota roll); 2025 (condemned the "guests" remark; set terms for the 2027 Naryn renewal, echoing "not negotiate with a recording").
* **Long Kurultai of 2022:** 19 days in August 2022, the longest on record. The Kökjal *biy* blocked IAC consent to the Naryn schedule renewal over flooded-pasture compensation; the Fergana Rim clans pressed for agreement. Three elders from clans with no stake in the reservoir, led by Biybolot Moldosh uulu, proposed that a fixed part of the ATR's Toktogul revenue go yearly to a fund for Toktogul basin families, run by Kökjal elders. The Kökjal took up the whip and stood aside; consent went to Tash-Kömür in early September 2022 (consistent with the "four months of talks").
* Wiki: Vol2 "Clans of the Autonomous Tribal Regions" and "Kurultai (Iradi Freedom Society)".

## Crime, Smuggling and the Criminal Code (October 2026)
* **Criminal Code of the Territory (1997):** territorial statute, still applied in the ISEZ (amended by *Mejlis* statute and executive decree since 2007); replaced the Soviet Iradi SSR code. Death penalty abolished by the territorial legislature in 1994; maximum penalty life imprisonment. Prosecutions by the office of the Attorney General. Other criminal laws: 1996 horse-slaughter ban, Extremist Activity Act 2008, Abduction for Marriage Act 2017.
* **Policing:** municipal police of Talas, Nurzhan and Iyık Janyl (city governments); district police detachments of the Ministry of Internal Security in the five districts; Gendarmerie for the line, installations and highways. All trained at the Talas Gendarmerie Academy. ATR: clan-appointed constables.
* **Organized crime:** 1990s racketeering over bazaars, fuel and car imports in Nurzhan and Talas; broken up in a 1997–1999 crackdown with US federal advisers. Since 2007 less visible and tied to patronage; car-theft rings selling into Kazakhstan; online fraud.
* **Narcotics:** Irad is a minor branch of the northern route for Afghan opiates (UNODC regional assessments); ISEZ seized 38 kg heroin in 2023. Wild cannabis in the lower Chu valley accounts for most drug arrests; online synthetic drugs in Talas since about 2020.
* **Horse theft:** *adat* requires return plus ninefold compensation (*toguz*); repeat thieves expelled; theft for slaughter worst. ISEZ 2009 amendment: aggravated offense, 3–10 years, up to 15 for registered breeding stock or theft for slaughter. Stolen horses are mostly driven into Kazakhstan, some to slaughterhouses.
* **Kyzyl-Jyldyz pedigree fraud:** March 2022 US buyer's retest of a palomino Akyel colt; breeder **Kanat Ormonbekov** (private Kyzyl-Jyldyz stud near Talas) substituted DNA samples for 14 foals (2017–2021) with technician **Aizada Sydykbekova** (Talas Veterinary Research Center). Syndicate struck 31 horses; US exports suspended four months. Talas District Court, June 2023: Ormonbekov 4 years (3 on appeal) for fraud and forgery of veterinary documents; Sydykbekova 2 years suspended. Mirlan Toktarov: internal review found nothing at syndicate farms; testing moved to a foreign lab in 2023.
* **Corruption:** Public Integrity Commission (Governor's office, 2014) prosecutes mainly junior and mid-level officials. Irad not in international corruption indices.
* **Prisons:** Corrections Directorate of the Ministry of Internal Security: Talas Pre-Trial Detention Center, Chu Correctional Colony (near Nurzhan), women's colony in Talas. About 4,900 prisoners in 2023 (~200 per 100,000 of 2.4 million), about a third on remand. No prisons in the ATR.
* **Statistics:** Ministry of Internal Security *Crime and Public Order Bulletin* 2023: about 21,400 offenses (theft 46%), 74 homicides (3.1 per 100,000), about 1,150 livestock thefts (about 210 involving horses). ISEZ only, unaudited.
* **Smuggling:** fuel (diesel 40–60% dearer in Chatkal villages, UNMI 2023), cigarettes diverted from the Talas Free Economic Trade Zone, hand-picked stibnite from abandoned tribal workings (surge after 2024 Chinese export controls), placer gold sold outside licensed buyers, unpapered Chongtor and Akyel horses, livestock avoiding fees and veterinary checks, medicines into the ATR. Routes: mountain trails (Kenkol Pass), Kariz galleries, false manifests at checkpoints, the ATR-held Kazakh frontier, the Kyzylkum Fringe and Bazar-Korgon market. Tribal traders called *soodager*. Barter rate reported by UNMI 2023: one fat-tailed sheep ≈ 150 liters of diesel. Clan councils acknowledge a customary levy on goods crossing their pastures.
* **Enforcement cases:** 17 Kariz shaft heads sealed in 2016; 2023 Qara-Chik bribery trial (six Gendarmerie sergeants convicted by the Talas District Court for passing fuel tankers; no officers charged); 2025 seizure of 46 tonnes of stibnite declared as fluorite at the Talas North Terminal.
* **Allegations (public layer only):** exile outlets allege, without documents, that diesel from Ruslan-linked Talas Air Base contracts reached the tribal market; FreeIrad.org claims Gendarmerie commanders control the diesel trade into the ATR. Both unverified; Ruslan denies improper interest.
* Wiki: Vol2 "Smuggling across the Qara-Chik Line", "Crime in Irad".

## Marriage in Irad (October 2026)
* **Terms:** marriage = *üylönüü*. *Kuda* = the two families as in-laws, made at a betrothal visit; *kalym* = bride price (livestock, cash, goods, often a riding horse); *sep* = dowry (shyrdak, white felt, töshök). Elders record the amounts as the basis of any separation settlement. Urban *kalym* is mostly a token payment.
* **Church rite:** East Syriac "crowning" (*burakha*): rings, a cup of wine, crowns or garlands; unconsecrated kümöch broken afterwards. In malka-keeping clans the bride brings leaven from her mother's house; losing it on the way is a bad omen. The bride wears white; white felt at the wedding yurt; the groom's mother puts the white *elechek* on her after the wedding; cattle kept out of sight.
* **Feast and horses:** wedding feast is the *toi* (beshbarmak/plov, boorsok, kymyz); a sheep is killed as food, not as an offering; horses never slaughtered. The bride traditionally arrives on a white horse led by the groom's kin; alaman-bayge, kyz kuu, free-for-all ulak at rural weddings. Urban feasts in banquet halls; many Russos have only civil registration plus a banquet.
* **Mixed marriages:** common in the cities (Russian, Koryo-saram; Mexican–Iradi couples in Semiz marry at the Spanish-language Mass, sometimes with the *lazo*). Catholic–Orthodox needs the bishop's permission; Catholic–Muslim (Fergana Rim) needs a dispensation and a promise to raise the children Catholic; some couples register only civilly.
* **Divorce:** church recognizes no divorce, only nullity declared by a church tribunal in Iyık Janyl. ISEZ civil divorce has existed since the territorial period. ATR customary separation by elders (dowry/kalym return, children); priests treat the couple as separated but not free to remarry.
* **ISEZ marriage law:** **Domestic Relations Act (1996)** of the Territorial Legislature: minimum age 18, civil registration by registry offices.
* **Bride kidnapping (*ala kachuu*):** present mainly in some ATR districts (Talas Alatau and Karatau zones) and parts of the rural ISEZ; rare in cities. Also covers consensual staged elopement (to avoid *kalym* or a parent's veto).
* **2021 survey** (Talas State University Institute of Sociology with the St. Luka Community Health Mission; 1,400 households; report 2022): 13% of married women under 50 in the surveyed parts of the Talas Alatau and Karatau zones said their marriage began with an abduction, about half against their will; under 2% in the three ISEZ cities. Caveats: small sample, refused access, under-reporting; IAC called it biased.
* **Church:** 2014 pastoral letter *On the Free Consent of Spouses* by the Episcopal Synod under Mar Ephrem: kidnapping "a sin against the sacrament and against the dignity of women"; priests must hear the bride's consent alone before crowning; a forced union is not a marriage.
* **Saikal Imanaly kyzy case (2015):** aged 19, from a Kara-Buura District village; escaped two days after her abduction; represented by Gulnara Sadykova (Legal Defense Guild); the court at Kyzyl-Adyr only fined the abductor, which led to the women's *Ak Jooluk* ("white headscarf") campaign.
* **Abduction for Marriage Act (2017)** of the Territorial Legislature (passed March 2017): 5–10 years' imprisonment, up to 12 if the victim is under 18; registry offices may not register such unions; prosecutions remain rare.
* **Adat position:** contested. Some clan councils (including the Tanıp) fine kidnappers (*aip*), annul the union and return the woman to her family; others accept it if the woman later stays. The IAC's 2022 Compendium records a precedent that a woman's refusal voids the union but leaves enforcement to each clan. Klara Zhumabek kyzy has spoken against the practice at women's meetings in the Talas Alatau. Wiki: Vol2 "Marriage in Irad".

## Iradi Calendar and Dialects of Iradi (October 2026)
* **Iradi calendar** (*Irad jyl sanagy*): Gregorian civil and church calendar (church since 1931); East Syriac seasons (Subara, Yalda–Denha, Great Fast *Uluu Savma*, Resurrection *Kyamta*, Apostles *Shlihe*, Summer *Kaita*, Elijah–Cross *Eliya–Salyba*, Moses, Dedication *Kudash Idta*). Archeparchy publishes the annual liturgical calendar *Tartip* (since 1993). Epiphany (6 Jan) water blessing; herders take the water home for winter. Wiki: Vol2 "Iradi calendar".
* **Fasts:** Iradi Christians say Syriac *savma* for a fast, never Muslim *orozo*. Rogation of the Ninevites = *Ninevi Savmasy*; rural households eat no meat, milk or butter, and many eat nothing until evening.
* **Feasts:** Holy Thursday = *Achytky Beyshembi* ("Leaven Thursday") in malka-keeping ATR clans. Feast of the Cross **14 September** (*Salyba Künü*), also the anniversary of *Orientalis Stella* and of the 1992 reconsecration; it closes the pilgrimage season and summer (saying: "after the Cross, no yurt on the *zhayloo*"). St. Gewargis (23 April), shown as a mounted saint in icons, is the usual date for the spring horse blessing in the Talas Alatau. Annunciation (25 March) = *Süyünchü Künü* ("day of good tidings"), promoted by rural clergy as the Christian spring feast and kept by some highland clans as the turn of the year.
* **Name days:** *at künü* (pun: *at* = "name" and "horse"; children get a ride or a toy horse). Celebrated on the feast of the baptismal saint; more important than birthdays in the countryside. The person attends the liturgy, receives the elders' *bata* and hosts a meal with boorsok and kümöch. Godparents are *suu ata* / *suu ene* ("water father/mother"), who traditionally promise a child a lamb or foal. All Janyls celebrate on 24 Aug. Russos keep Russian *imeniny*; Zoomers have revived name-day greetings online.
* **Pastoral year:** spring *köch batasy* (migration blessing, April–May, with water, milk and arsha; Mazar-Too offerings on the Northern Steppe); *kymyz muryndyk* (late May–June); autumn return in September after the Janyl and Cross feasts, ending with a camp thanksgiving liturgy.
* **Months:** Russian-derived Gregorian names (*yanvar*…) in speech and in ISEZ documents. Traditional Kyrgyz-type names (*Üchtün ayy, Birdin ayy, Jalgan kuran, Chyn kuran, Bugu, Kulja, Teke, Bash oona, Ayak oona, Toguzdun ayy, Jetinin ayy, Beshtin ayy*) are used in the ATR, almanacs and the Iradi press. August is also called *Janyl ayy* in folk usage.
* **Müchöl** (twelve-animal cycle): used to count age, not as a belief. The year turns in spring traditionally and on 1 Jan in urban almanacs. The Cow year is unlucky (bovine omens) and many highlanders call it *arkar jyly* (Year of the Argali); some avoid weddings in it. The Horse year (*jylky jyly*) is auspicious; the current one began in 2026. Cycle-completing ages (12, 24, 36…) are dangerous; people give away clothing and ask a priest's blessing. The church tolerates the custom unless it is used for fortune-telling.
* **Nooruz dispute:** in the 1993 debate, Fergana Rim Muslim deputies and the Talas intelligentsia supported the holiday; several Synod members called it "a Persian feast, kept by Muslims". The Act kept it with the alternative name *navbahor*. **1997 Synod declaration:** a folk custom the faithful may keep, the official position since. Muslims celebrate it fully; urban Iradis and Zoomers keep it as steppe heritage; Russos treat it as a day off; ATR clans hold races, but some highland elders prefer *Süyünchü Künü*. A proposal in the 2008 revision to replace it with 25 March was rejected by the Mejlis.
* **Dialects of Iradi** (*Irad tilinin sheveleri*): classification from the Iradi SSR Academy of Sciences expeditions (1952–83) and **Kenjegul Abakirova**'s *Dialect Atlas of the Iradi Language* (1984). Talas (middle valley) is the standard. **Russocha** ("the Russo way") is Nurzhan Iradi–Russian code-switching: Russian discourse words (*koroche*, *nu*), Russian numerals, Russian verb + *et-* (*zvonit et-*). Speakers who mix the two are mocked as **chala-Irad** ("half-Iradi"). Young Talas speakers mix in English in a similar way. Wiki: Vol2 "Dialects of Iradi".
* **Tribal dialects:** fewest Russian loans (*unaa* "car", originally "pack animal"), evidential *-ptyr*, epic diction. Subgroups: highland (Talas Alatau, Chatkal) and northern steppe/Karatau (/ʒ/ for *j*; *sh* for *ch* in some words, as in Kazakh). **Chatkal:** Syriac church words in everyday speech (*kashysh* "priest", *idta* "yurt chapel", *ene achytky*); *tag* "mountain" kept in songs and place names.
* **Fergana Rim dialects:** Uzbek bilingualism, *ä* kept as a separate vowel, weak rounding harmony, Uzbek words (*dada*, *juda*, *hozir*, *dokon*, *kasalkhona*); Muslim speakers use Arabic religious vocabulary.
* **1995 Latin Orthography Revision:** one letter *j* covers /dʒ/ and steppe /ʒ/; *ä* kept for Fergana speech and loanwords. **Prestige:** Talas speech is "correct" (2022 survey); young people call highland speech the most "real"; Russocha is stigmatized but linked to Nurzhan's oil wealth and status.
* **Contact:** in Semiz, local Iradi uses *jefe* (foreman) and *tortilla*; Semiz Spanish uses *som*, *aksakal*, *bazar*, *marshrutka*. Korean *kuksi* has entered Iradi and Nurzhan Russian; young people use K-pop slang.

## Glacier Retreat and Water Stress (October 2026)
* **Wiki article:** "Glacier retreat in Irad" (Iradi *Irad möngülörünün kyskaryshy*), Geography & Nature.
* **Magnitudes:** upper Talas basin glaciers lost about a fifth of their area 1970–2020 (unchanged); Kashka-Suu glaciers about a quarter 1970–2018; small, low Chatkal cirque glaciers lost up to about a third. The 2014–2018 Talas basin inventory (Talas River Hydrological Monitoring Service, 2019) counted **268 glaciers** in the Iradi part of the basin. Glacier melt supplies an estimated **30–40% of August flow** in high Talas Alatau tributaries. Talas basin "peak water" is projected for the **2030s–2040s**; some small Chatkal catchments may already be past it.
* **Glaciologist:** **Nazira Isaeva**, Talas State University / Manas Peak observatory, author of the 2024 peak-water projections (no further biography).
* **2016 GLOF:** on the night of **22 July 2016** the moraine dam of **Muz-Köl** (about 3,300 m, head of the Muz-Suu, tribal side) partly failed. Herders radioed a warning; the flood reached the valley about three hours later, washed out a road bridge and one of the Muz-Suu's two canal intakes, covered about 40 ha with gravel and damaged 14 houses in **Tash-Bulak** (village, Talas District, ISEZ). No deaths. It led to the observatory's 2020 hazard assessment and to sirens and water-level sensors in the Muz-Suu and Urmaral valleys, maintained by clan wardens on the tribal side.
* **2021 drought:** August 2021 flow at the Talas border gauge about half the long-term mean; Kazakh complaint of receiving less than its 50%, settled technically by agreeing to count Kyzyl-Adyr Reservoir releases. Canal rotations in Kara-Buura District; NAI feed-price accusations; Zhetysu shift cuts (2021 and 2023).
* **2023 drought:** Toktogul fell close to the minimum reserve level (2008 remains the record low). After Uzbek observers' protests the commission approved an extra August release; the ISEZ trimmed electricity exports in winter 2023–24; Kazakh customs slowed clearance at northern crossings for about two weeks.
* **Policy:** ISEZ **Irrigation Modernization Programme** (from 2018: canal lining, laser leveling, drip irrigation; budget unpublished). **Executive Directive 2022/14** (water-saving plans from large users in dry years; limits new rice paddies on the lower Chu). Analysts urge glacier projections be built into the 2027 Naryn schedule.
* **Folk/church:** drought rite *jamgyr tilöö* ("asking for rain") at mazars and springs: white ribbons, milk and kymyz poured on stones, juniper smoke, no animal sacrifice. The Synod asked parishes to add prayers for rain in July 2021 and in 2023; more pilgrims came to springs such as Maryam-Bulak.

## Iradi Diasporas: Russia and the Returnee Elite (October 2026)
* **Iradis in Russia** (Iradi *Orusiyadagy Iraddar*): two layers: (1) 1990s emigrants, about 250,000 people (mostly ethnic Russians and Ukrainians, incl. ex-SRI officials, doctors, teachers) left for Russia 1992–2000, many to the Urals and Western Siberia, some under Russia's compatriot resettlement program; now Russian citizens, mostly Orthodox; (2) labor migrants from ISEZ cities, mainly Russian-speaking men aged 20–45 from Nurzhan and Talas, after 2007. ATR migrants go to southern Kazakhstan instead (they rarely speak Russian). Plus the **Russo second generation**: Russian-schooled children of Russo families who went to Russian universities from the late 1990s and stayed; most are Russian citizens and speak little Iradi. Ruslan Toktarov (Omsk, 1995–99) is the best-known Iradi of that generation to study in Russia, though he returned. Wiki: "Iradis in Russia".
* **Numbers:** Russian statistics do not count Iradis separately. Talas State University Institute of Sociology 2024 survey: 70,000–120,000 ISEZ residents working in Russia at peak season; Russia is the second destination after Kazakhstan. Centers: Moscow and the Moscow region, St. Petersburg, Yekaterinburg, Novosibirsk, and the West Siberian oil towns (Surgut, Nizhnevartovsk; *vakhta* shift work; Nurzhan Polytechnic graduates).
* **Documents:** the ISEZ has issued **"Republic of Irad" passports since 2009**; Russia and most states do not recognize them. Territorial-era US national status ended December 2006. **Kyrgyzstan** issues passports to Iradis who prove birth or long residence in Irad (most readily); **Kazakhstan** also issues them (mainly to applicants with family ties there); **Uzbekistan** rarely. Kyrgyz and Kazakh passports (EAEU) let migrants work in Russia **without a patent**, so most Iradi migrants hold Kyrgyz passports. Older emigrants hold Russian citizenship. The ISEZ does not forbid other documents; the DPI calls it "a practical necessity", and young Iradi nationalists criticize it.
* **Remittances:** sent via Russian and Kazakh money-transfer services to Talasbank accounts and its SMS service, paid out in dollars or som (5:1), routed through Almaty correspondent banks. After 2022, sanctions made the correspondent banks stricter on Russian-origin payments and fees rose. 2024 survey estimate: US$250–400 million a year (~1.5–2% of official GDP), well below transfers from Kazakhstan (inside the canon total of 5–8% of GDP).
* **Community:** **Syro-Iradi Mission of St. Yuhanna, Moscow** (founded 2004): monthly East Syriac liturgy in Iradi in a Latin-rite parish church; priest sent from the eparchy of Nurzhan; under the Latin Archdiocese of the Mother of God at Moscow. **Irad Fellow-Countrymen's Association** (*Irad Jerdeshteri*; Russian *zemlyachestvo*), Moscow 1996, branches in St. Petersburg and Yekaterinburg: legal advice, repatriating the bodies of migrants who die in Russia, a Victory Day gathering and a New Year concert; the association says the Bekzatov Foundation has supported some events.
* **Politics:** 2024 survey: 71% of migrants support EAEU accession (about a third of ISEZ city residents in 2023); many back restoring Russian as an official language; leaning Russo/Bekzatov. **The ISEZ has no out-of-country voting** (Iradis abroad vote in person at home). The Moscow association condemned the 2025 "sale" remarks.
* **Since 2022:** after Russia's September 2022 partial mobilization, the *Talas Herald* (4 Oct 2022) reported young Russian-citizen Iradis returning to Nurzhan or sending their families there, and migrants approached by recruiters offering contract service; the ISEZ Department of Public Information advised residents working in Russia to sign no military contracts. Russia's tighter migration rules from 2024 (registration, language tests, expulsions) have reduced seasonal departures from Nurzhan and turned more migrants toward Kazakhstan.
* **Returnees (*kaytkandar*, "those who came back"):** Iradi Americans and US-educated Iradis who returned after 1992; much of the territorial DPI, civil service, Talasbank/Attelkom management and the commercial bar. Waves: Chicago émigrés and their children as advisers and DPI founders (1992–94); territorial-scholarship graduates (examples: **Bakyt Orozbekov**, Harvard LL.M. 1996; **Azamat Temirov**, World Bank 1997–2006, back 2007). **Aidar Maksat studied at the LSE**, and **Alikhan Toktarov studied in Bishkek and at Regent's College, London**: neither was US-educated, though both are often grouped with the returnees. Zara Toktarova is at Stanford.
* **Alatau Heights** (colloquially ***Amerikanka***, a Russian-style nickname like *Meksikanka*): a gated suburb on the foothill slopes south-east of central Talas, built 1998–2005 by American and local developers for base contractors and returnees (detached houses, cul-de-sacs, a shopping plaza). **Talas American School**: founded 1994 for the children of contractors and diplomats; US curriculum, English-language; most graduates go on to US universities, and many stay there.
* **Tension with locally rooted elites:** after 2007 the New DPI promoted Soviet-trained officers, officials and Russo managers; the returnees around Orozbekov were linked with the DRI and the 2007 privatization scandals, and several left Irad. Russos and nationalists caricature the *kaytkandar* as rootless and poor in Iradi; returnees say they built the territory's institutions. Suspicion grew after the 2025 Trump remarks (cf. Jason Ilyasov).

## Mexicans in Irad and the District of Semiz (October 2026)
* **Community size:** more than 10,000 Mexicans (canon), the largest Mexican community in Central Asia; community leaders estimate 12,000–14,000 people with at least one Mexican parent (no official count). About 6,000 by 2006.
* **Origins:** SuKarne (based in Culiacán, Sinaloa) sent about 120 managers, vets and engineers in 1998–99. Families and skilled workers followed in 2000–01, recruited in **Sinaloa, Durango and Sonora**. Conagra brought a smaller group of Mexican and Mexican-American supervisors from its plants in Colorado and Nebraska. Later arrivals came mostly through family ties.
* **2007:** during the Popular Front's investment of Talas (from 22 April 2007), SuKarne evacuated about 1,400 women and children on **Iradiya** charters from the Talas Air Base aprons. Most men stayed. St. Yusup parish was a shelter, and SuKarne cold-store beef was handed out in the western corridor. Community bodies stayed neutral. Three Semiz residents died during the investment (per the *Talas Herald*), one a Mexican maintenance engineer. Semiz organized night watches and escaped the looting seen in the center. Most families returned after the ceasefire.
* **Legal status:** territorial employment permits sponsored by employers. Mexican workers did not get the US national status of permanent territorial residents, and hopes for children born in Irad ended in December 2006. The ISEZ issues renewable residence permits; a **2010 regulation** added a long-term residence card after 10 years. Most keep Mexican citizenship. Consular services come from Mexican missions abroad; a mobile consular team visits Talas about twice a year. Children with an Iradi parent are registered as ISEZ residents and can vote; Mexican-born residents cannot.
* **Work:** about a third work at the two beef plants. Others work in feed, reefer trucking, veterinary practice and labs, or run small businesses (tortillerías, taquerías, groceries, bakery, workshops). The second generation is moving into teaching, nursing, translation and office work.
* **Religion:** Latin-rite Catholics cared for by the Syro-Iradi Church, with no separate Latin parish. Roman-rite Spanish Mass every Sunday at the Syro-Iradi **Church of St. Yusup the Worker** (*Emgekchi Yusup Chirköösü*), Semiz, consecrated 2005 (community funds plus a SuKarne gift), served by priests lent by the Diocese of Culiacán. Its **Guadalupe chapel** was approved in 2006 by the then Bishop of Talas (now Archbishop Mar Ephrem) with the Synod's consent, despite some clergy objecting to Marian imagery as a Latin import. The image was painted by Iradi icon painters and framed with the Iradi-cross motif. The 12 December feast draws Iradis too.
* **Day of the Dead / Fallen Rider:** 1–2 November. Ofrendas at home; graves in **Semiz cemetery** (Orthodox, Korean and Catholic sections) decorated with marigolds from imported seed, which Iradis call "Mexican flowers". Merged with the urban Syro-Iradi All Souls' (2 November) grave visits: Iradi spouses add white ribbons and milk. **Since 2008** the evening procession is led by a riderless horse with a reversed saddle, first in memory of the 2007 dead. In mixed families a horse is often led behind the bier at funerals.
* **Language:** second generation trilingual (Spanish/Iradi/English), many with some Russian. Semiz Spanish uses Iradi *ata* (grandfather); Talas slang has taken *compa*.
* **Food:** yellow "Talas tortillas" (canon); beef tacos are a Talas student food.
* **Attitudes:** *Meksikanka* is now mostly affectionate. Early slur *uychu* ("cowherd"), tied to the cattle omens. Shared Catholicism and love of horses helped acceptance.
* **First Mexican-Iradi city councillor:** **Andrés Ochoa Kaparov** (born Talas 2000; father a SuKarne vet from Culiacán, mother an Iradi teacher; formerly a SuKarne interpreter). Elected to the Talas city council in **November 2023 on the DPI list** for the western Talas constituency, which includes Semiz. Critics called it a DPI gesture to the western corridor.
* **2025:** after the Trump remarks, many Semiz residents joined the Talas marches, some carrying Mexican and Iradi flags together; the parish held a Mass "for Irad". A few residents reported being mistaken for Americans and abused.
* **Semiz history:** founded **1958** as the fattening station of a Virgin Lands beef sovkhoz (Russian, Ukrainian and Korean settlers, timber barracks). Enlarged in **1974** into the **Talas State Cattle-Fattening Complex** (about 40,000 head/year), with five-storey panel blocks, school, club and water tower. Population about 6,500 in 1989. Closed in 1994; renamed *Semiz* by the Talas city council in **1994** under the Toponymic Heritage Statute (1993). About a third of flats empty by 1998 after Russian emigration. Land passed to TAEZ in 1998.
* **Colonia SuKarne:** in 1999–2003 SuKarne refurbished blocks and built about 400 courtyard houses south of the old settlement. The name shortened to *la Colonia* and spread to the whole district. Private houses have been built on former feedlot land to the south since the 2010s.
* **Semiz geography:** about 9 km west of central Talas; Trans-Talas Highway on the north edge; TAEZ sidings and SuKarne feedlot to the west. Three parts: old settlement (north, a few barracks), the blocks (center, former club now community center, **water tower** landmark), la Colonia (south, flat-roofed courtyard houses in northern-Mexican style, brightly painted).
* **Landmarks:** St. Yusup church; **Semiz market** (*Semiz bazary*, *el mercado*; daily, largest on Saturdays); about a dozen tortillerías (first in 2000); **Lienzo Charro de Semiz** (opened 2009); Semiz cemetery.
* **Joint horse festival:** *Eki Eldin At Oyunu* ("horse games of two peoples"), each September near 16 September at the Lienzo Charro. Charro roping and women's *escaramuza*; Iradi kyz kuu, alaman-bayge and an ulak tartysh demonstration. **Bull events are held on a separate day** because of cattle omens. Sanctioned by the National Equestrian Sports Directorate since 2012.
* **Population of Semiz:** about 23,000 (2018 census). A 2021 Talas State University survey found about 40% of households with at least one Mexican-born member, about 35% Iradi, and the rest Russian, Ukrainian or Koryo-saram.
* **Transport:** municipal buses and marshrutkas along the Trans-Talas Highway, about 25 minutes to the center; **city bus route 14** runs to the Old City; SuKarne and Conagra run shift buses; no passenger rail.
* **Names:** official *Semiz*; Russos *Otkormochny*; Mexicans *la Colonia*; Iradis *Meksikanka*. Never "Nuevo Talas". Wiki: Vol2 "Mexicans in Irad", "Semiz".

## Iradi Literature and Kemel Ishakov (October 2026)
* **Overview:** Iradi literature = oral (epic *Janyl Baatyr*, koshok, ırda, sanjyra, bata, *The Black Blizzard*), church manuscripts (Syriac, Sogdian, Christian Turkic; 18th-c. Arabic-script vita of Janyl), then modern written literature in Iradi, Russian and English. Wiki: "Iradi literature".
* **19th century:** first printed book by an Iradi author: proverbs, riddles and a Kokand-era verse chronicle compiled by deacon-teacher **Yakup Satybaldiev** (1858–1919), printed in Tashkent, 1897.
* **Purged first generation:** Latin-script literary monthly ***Jañy Jol*** ("New Road", 1928–1936), edited by poet **Ernis Tashtemirov** (1901–1937), author of the first Latin-script school reader (1929, illustrated by Yusup Ayyubov); shot in Talas, November 1937. Short-story writer **Aisuluu Bayalinova** (1906–1941), collective-farm women; arrested 1937, died in a labor camp. Both reprinted 1989.
* **Soviet institutions:** Union of Writers of the Iradi SSR, founded 1934; socialist realism; Christian subjects banned. Thaw generation from 1956.
* **Samizdat:** hand-copied prayers, hymns and lives of St. Janyl (Cyrillic or Syriac script); *The Black Blizzard* circulated in handwritten/typed copies until 1992; Iradi American Association in Chicago printed émigré poetry and church texts.
* **Territorial period:** English becomes a literary language. *The Last Pass* (English translation of Ishakov) published by **Lakeshore Press, Chicago, 1993**. **Elnura Kudaiberdieva** (b. 1979), English-language novel ***Siege Winter*** (2005, New York publisher) on the 1992 siege of Talas. Tilek Sharshenov's nationalist Iradi poetry.
* **Revival since 2014:** new urban poetry mainly in Iradi, first on social media and at Mor-Wave-linked readings; Aigul Omurova as a model. **Saira Kurmanbek kyzy** (b. 1998), collection ***Suu Jolu*** (*Water Road*, 2020). **Temirlan Omurbekov** (b. 1994, Russian-speaking Nurzhan upbringing), Iradi novel ***Mikrorayon*** (2021). *Jarchy* has a poetry page.
* **Censorship:** publishers and printers register with the Department of Public Information; writers avoid civil wars, governing family, Governor's absence. **Arsha Books** (small Talas publisher) had registration suspended in 2024 after an anthology of poems on the 2023 Student Protests (DPI: "licensing irregularity"; HRW: censorship). Jer-Suu writings banned under Extremist Activity Act 2008.
* **ATR oral poetry:** bards sing the epic at weddings, memorials, Feast of St. Janyl; improvised ***aytysh*** poetic duels at the August Kurultai on the Aksay Plateau; new laments about 2007 circulate on audio files and memory cards across the line.
* **Altyn-Zhylky:** Ishakov first lifetime-award winner (1996); **Kemel Ishakov Prize** for a first book in Iradi since 2010.
* **Kemel Ishakov** (Iradi *Kemel Ishak uulu*; Russian *Кемел Исхаков*; 9 Oct 1930 Arsha-Say – 2 Feb 2009 Talas; buried Arsha-Say): national novelist, wrote in Iradi and Russian. Born in **Arsha-Say**, village on a side stream of the Talas below the Talas Alatau, Talas District; Christian collective-farm horse herders; grandmother survived the 1916 flight. Father **Ishak** prosecuted for "sabotage" after the 1944 Wartime Mobilization Strike, eight years in camp, returned 1953. Talas Pedagogical Institute (Iradi and Russian philology), 1952; village teacher; wrote for *Iradyn Chyny*; editor at republican state publishing house from 1960. Chaired Union of Writers of the Iradi SSR 1986–1991. Signed March 2007 open letter (writers, actors, clergy) asking both sides to spare Talas. House-museum in Arsha-Say since 2012.
* **Ishakov's works:** ***Kök Jal*** (*Grey Mane*, 1959, story; old herder hides a grey stallion through a winter, understood as about 1944). ***Akyrky Ashuu*** (*The Last Pass*, 1964, novella; girl **Bermet** and her grandfather in the 1916 flight; clan reaches Xinjiang, most die, Bermet returns alone 1918; censors cut final chapter with the rider in white, restored 1990; own Russian version in a Moscow monthly 1965; State Prize of the Iradi SSR 1966; 20+ languages; Talas State Academic Drama Theatre staging 1967 with Almaz Sarybaev as the clan elder's son; critics compare *Rise of Glory*'s 1916 sequence, uncredited; IradOne TV adaptation 2016). ***Ünsüz Koñguroo*** (*The Silent Bell*; teacher discovers the village church bell buried by its priest in 1937; cut Russian version Moscow 1971, Iradi original refused until 1989; travel ban for several years). ***Kara Buka*** (*The Black Bull*, 1977; Iradi herder on a Virgin Lands cattle sovkhoz vs Morkhan omens). ***Ak Bulut*** (*White Cloud*, 2003; itinerant priest's yurt liturgies in the Chatkal valley).
* **Not adapted by Sadikov or Karimov:** their filmographies are unchanged.

## Football in Irad and the Kara-Ak Derby (October 2026)
* **Governing body:** Football Federation of Irad (*Irad Futbol Federatsiyasy*, FFI), founded Talas 1993; president since 2012 **Kanat Ermekov** (former Tulpar defender). Runs the **Irad League** (*Irad Ligasy*; ISEZ clubs only; first season 1994; 2007 cancelled; triple round robin March–November), the eight-club **First League** (*Birinchi Liga*) and the Irad Cup. 1993 naming rules required Iradi or neutral club names. IradOne Sport holds league rights; IradOdin airs Russian commentary of Neftchi matches.
* **Soviet era:** **Neftyanik Kobozev** (Iradneft, founded 1958), Second League Central Asian zone 1971–1991, First League 1984; Koryo-saram striker **Vadim Ten** scored 141 league goals (1975–87). **Dinamo Talas** (interior ministry) and **Lokomotiv Kobozev** (railway) played mostly in the republican championship.
* **2026 Irad League (10 clubs, titles 1994–2025):** Neftchi Nurzhan (NurGaz) 13; Tulpar Talas (Talas Commercial Development Group, Adil Toktarov, since 2011) 11; Temirjolchu Nurzhan (Irad Temir Jolu; ex-Lokomotiv) 3; Attelkom Talas 2; Universitet Talas (TSU) 1; Surma Chatkal (IARMC, Sary-Bulak District) 1; Dastgoh Nurzhan (Heavy Machine Plant), FC Iyık Janyl, Kara-Buura (Kyzyl-Adyr), Naryn Tash-Kömür 0. **Deportivo Semiz** (from the Mexican amateur league) in the First League since 2018.
* **Territorial era:** Liberty Youth Soccer League (Talas, 1994; American Christian charities and air-base families); Talas Youth Soccer Academy (1997; American college coaches); girls' school teams; basketball, softball, flag and tackle American football remain minority sports.
* **International status:** 1996 AFC application (citing Guam and other US-territory members) deferred without decision after objections by Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan and Kyrgyzstan; 2008 application not taken up (FIFA requires a recognized independent state). Ermekov called the deferral "the theft of a generation". FFI joined **CONIFA in 2014**; played its World Football Cup 2016 and 2018. Friendlies vs Iradi American (Chicago) selections, Kazakh clubs, US service teams; annual **Irad Day friendly** (since 1995) suspended 2025 after the Trump remarks. National team: white and crimson.
* **Stadiums:** **Silk Road Arena**, Talas, 21,000 (1956 Dinamo Stadium, rebuilt 2014 by Talas Commercial Development Group; Tulpar and national team). **NurGaz Stadium**, Nurzhan, 16,500 (1958 Neftyanik Stadium, renamed 1998). Irad Central Sports Complex hosts the national futsal cup.
* **ATR:** almost no organized football; informal village play; UNMI youth tournaments near the Qara-Chik line since 2012.
* **Women:** six-club Women's League since 2009, dominated by Tulpar and Universitet Talas; national team captain since 2019 **Begimai Asanova** (midfielder).
* **Kara-Ak derby** (*Kara-Ak darbisi*, "Black–White"): Neftchi (black and orange, fans still say "Neftyanik"; president since 2009 **Rustam Bekzatov**) vs Tulpar (white and crimson; named for the epic winged horse). The two clubs have won 24 of the 31 titles; Neftchi won 8 of the first 12, Tulpar 6 of 10 since 2015. Head to head to end 2025: 118 league matches, Neftchi 46, Tulpar 43, draws 29. Study (2021): 71% of Neftchi season-ticket holders speak Russian at home vs 18% at Tulpar.
* **Supporters' groups:** Neftchi: **Vyshka** ("Derrick", 1999, east stand) and **Kobozevtsy** (2006). Tulpar: **Jolbors** ("Grey Mane", 2004) and **Azat Tribuna** ("Free Stand", TSU students, 2012). Tulpar fans taunt Neftchi as "Morkhan's herd"; Vyshka embraces black.

## Architecture of Irad (October 2026)
* **Overview:** The ISEZ has planning codes and heritage law; the ATR builds by custom. Earthquake resistance shapes building because of the Talas–Fergana Fault. Wiki: Architecture of Irad (Vol2, new).
* **Russian colonial Talas:** the grid town east of the citadel keeps single-story brick houses with iron-roofed porches and carved wooden window frames.
* **Kobozev/Nurzhan:** the former **House of Soviets** (1929–1932, constructivist; a stepped concrete block with horizontal window bands) on the central square of the 1927 plan now houses the Nurzhan city administration. Stalinist granite neoclassicism after 1945 (Azatlık Prospect, Polytechnic main building 1956; also the Hall of Justice, Talas). The **Palace of Metallurgists** by Bolotbek Bekzhanov dates from **1964**.
* **Seismic codes:** after the 1946 Chatkal earthquake most of the Iradi SSR was put in Soviet seismic zones 8–9. Khrushchyovka five-story blocks had reinforced-concrete ring beams; nine-story frame-panel blocks came in the 1970s. In the 1992 Suusamyr quake most panel blocks stood with little damage, while mudbrick houses cracked. The **Territorial Building Code of 1996** was adapted from the US Uniform Building Code.
* **Talas Philharmonic Hall** (1971–1974): home of the Talas Symphony Orchestra; architect **Erkin Sydykbekov**; a round concrete hall under a ribbed shell gathered into a central skylight, nicknamed *Tündük*; a foyer mosaic of epic riders. Listed in **2021** after a campaign by architects and the *Talas Herald*.
* **Territorial period:** strip malls, fast food and bonded warehouses along the highway from western Talas to the air base; gated contractor estates such as **Cottonwood Estates** (1997; wooden-frame houses with lawns). Attelkom's glass headquarters on Communications Square, **2002**. Boz-Korgon's architecture is known only from tenders and satellite imagery.
* **Since 2011:** Talas skyline along the central avenue (Alikhan's and Adil Toktarov's companies). The tallest is the **Altyn Tower** (2016, 27 stories, base-isolated).
* **Semiz:** Soviet five-story blocks of Otkormochny plus brightly painted Mexican-style courtyard houses.
* **ATR:** stone-and-adobe *kyshtoo* winter houses with flat earth roofs, felt-lined; yurts put up beside them in summer. Villages damaged in 2007 were rebuilt in stone without engineering supervision (raised in UN shelter surveys).
* **Churches since 1992:** most show the Iradi cross. There are two models: Latin-style (nave, pews, spire, statues; e.g. **Church of St. Tomas, Talas, 1996**, funded by American Catholic charities) and revived East Syriac (domed hall, bema, madbaha behind a walnut screen, ganch, no statues; some orient the sanctuary toward Polaris, *kuble*). The Synod's **2011 Guidelines on the Building and Furnishing of Churches** recommend the East Syriac model but do not require it.
* **Heritage:** the 1997 ordinance protects the citadel quarter, but Russian and Soviet buildings outside it have little protection. A **1910 merchant house near Bazar-i Kalan was demolished for a hotel in 2018** (*Talas Herald*).

## Iradi Language Revival (renamed, October 2026)
* **Rename:** the wiki article "Iradi Zoomer Movement" is now **"Iradi language revival"** (Iradi *tilge kaytuu*, "return to the language", from a 2016 *Talas Herald* essay series). Scholars treat it as a generational pattern, not a movement. "Zoomers" (Iradi *zumerler*, often ironic) survives only as the English-media nickname for the generation (born c. 1997–2012); generational taxonomy elsewhere in the lore is unchanged.
