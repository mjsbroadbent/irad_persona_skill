# Irad New Lore and Updates

## Demographic Correction: True Population
* **Wiki Error:** The wiki states the population is 4,626,000 (based on a 2018 estimate).
* **Correction:** The actual population is approximately 6.2 million.
* **Reason for Error:** The 4.6 million figure was sourced directly from the DPI's official state census. Independent demographic researchers and satellite monitoring have since proven that the DPI intentionally suppressed and undercounted the rural population in the Autonomous Tribal Regions to dilute the perceived political influence of the Iradi Freedom Society (IAC).

## Political Geography: The True Seat of Power
* **Wiki Error:** Talas is listed strictly as the political capital of the governorate. 
* **Correction:** While Talas remains the *de jure* capital and the public-facing administrative hub for foreign corporations, the actual *de facto* seat of DPI power has relocated. Governor Erlan Toktarov and his inner circle operate out of "Boz-Korgon" (Grey Fortress), a heavily fortified military-industrial compound 40 kilometers [24.8 miles] east of Nurzhan.
* **Reason for Error:** State media (IradOne) and official territorial documents maintain the legal fiction that Talas is the operational capital to appease Western corporate partners. In reality, rising unrest and security concerns in Talas forced Toktarov to quietly retreat to Boz-Korgon in early 2023.

## The Toktarov Family Dynamics
* **New Lore:** Erlan Toktarov has five sons and one daughter.
* **Ruslan and Alikhan:** The two eldest sons, currently engaged in an increasingly public succession friction.
* **Timur, Adil, and Bekzat:** The three middle sons. They maintain a lower public profile and focus exclusively on managing the family's business endeavors and monopolies within the ISEZ.
* **Zara:** Erlan's youngest child and only daughter. Officially pursuing a doctorate in education from Stanford University.
* **Aidar Maksat:** Erlan's son-in-law (married to Zara) and Lieutenant Governor since 2019. He manages the day-to-day operations of the ISEZ during Toktarov's absence.

## National Vexillology: The Official Flag of Irad
* **Field:** Solid crimson red field, continuing the revolutionary tradition of the 1916 Semirechye uprising and the banner of the ISSR.
* **Central Stallion:** A white horse in gait representing national equestrian heritage, the Chongtor and Akyel breeds, and the historical belief in Irad as the geo-genetic birthplace of the domesticated horse.
* **The Guiding Star:** A five-pointed star positioned directly above the stallion, representing Polaris (*Irad* / *Irad's star*).
* **The Inverted Horseshoe (Fallen Rider Tradition):** Positioned on the hoist side of the stallion. It embodies the steppe cavalry custom of the "Fallen Rider," where downward-facing tack signifies an unmounted warrior who perished in battle. On the national flag, it serves as an official, permanent memorial to the martyrs of Irad—specifically commemorating those lost in the 1916 Semirechye Revolt, Soviet purges, and the civil wars.
* **Arched Inscription:** Inscribed in ancient Orkhon (Old Turkic) script, translating directly to *"Be Free"* in the Iradi language.
* **Wheat Wreath:** Two stylized golden ears of wheat bordering the lower half of the roundel, retaining traditional Soviet-era republican heraldic motifs while symbolizing agricultural life in the river valleys.

## Naming Conventions & De-Russification
* **New Lore:** Starting in the 1990s, Irad underwent a large-scale shift away from Russian-style names. Soviet-era geographical designations are considered obsolete. 
* **City of Nurzhan (formerly Kobozev):** The city of Kobozev was originally named after the real-world Soviet figure Pyotr Kobozev. Following the fall of the ISSR, Amanbek Alimbekov officially renamed the city. During his speech, Alimbekov declared that Nurzhan would be a "guiding light of industry" leading Irad to a new dawn after the ruin of the civil war. "Nurzhan" does not refer to a person; it is a Kipchak-Turkic word translating to "Light of Life." *(Note: Any past apocryphal references to a "Dmitry Kobozev" are strictly non-canon and should be ignored).*
* **Personal Names:** Personal names reflect the deep political and geographic divide. Urban populations within the ISEZ often retain Russian surname suffixes (-ov, -ev, -ova). Conversely, tribal-aligned populations in the rural regions have entirely reverted to traditional Turkic patronymics (-uulu for "son of" and -kyzy for "daughter of").

## Infrastructure & Cold War Intelligence: The Kok-Tash Kariz System
* **New Lore:** Beneath the eastern Tien Shan foothills lies the Kok-Tash Kariz system, an ancient irrigation network expanded during the Kara-Khanid Khanate.
* **Cold War History:** Sections were covertly converted into logistical conduits during the Cold War through CIA-funded operations supporting local Christian and tribal anti-Soviet cells.
* **Public / Journalistic Framing:** In public histories and archaeological surveys, the Kariz system is cataloged as a Silk Road hydraulic marvel. Its use for smuggling by the IAC is reported as an opportunistic exploitation of ancient caves; historical CIA funding remains unacknowledged in official records.

## Religious Figures: Janyl, Mother of Irad
* **New Lore:** The pseudo-historical figure Janyl is revered as the "Mother of Irad." According to tradition, she received a vision of Jesus in the form of the White Rider, who led her to the site of the First Iradi Church (known as the First Church of Janyl). Her historical existence is widely disputed by modern academics, and the actual location of the original church remains unknown.

## Mythology & Superstition: Morkhan and Bovine Omens
* **New Lore:** Morkhan was a pagan god from Iradi pre-Christian beliefs. He is traditionally depicted as a large, dark man with the head of a bull.
* **Christian Syncretism:** Within Iradi Christian tradition, Morkhan became directly associated with Satan.
* **Cultural Impact:** Because of this association, cows and bovines are frequently linked to dark omens or ill fortune in modern Iradi culture, contrasting sharply with their revered view of horses.

## Cultural Addition: Equine Obsession and Dietary Taboos
* **New Lore:** Iradi culture is intensely horse-obsessed, driven by the belief that Irad is the geo-genetic birthplace of the modern domesticated horse. While Iradis regularly consume horse milk products, the consumption of horsemeat is strictly forbidden. Culturally, there is a deep-seated prejudice that views eating horsemeat as a barbarous, uniquely Muslim practice. This taboo carries a stigma roughly equivalent to Western unease surrounding the consumption of dog meat.

## Culinary Traditions and Unique Foods
* **Koryoin Laghman:** A unique variation of the traditional Central Asian laghman noodle dish developed by Korean immigrants in Irad. It is distinguished by the addition of sesame and kimchi, fusing local staples with Korean flavors.
* **Jasil Drink:** A unique Iradi beverage dating back to the Soviet era, made with a vibrant green herb syrup. Its primary flavor profile is tarragon, but it contains various other herbs depending on the regional or household version.
* **Beet Suzma:** A traditional Iradi variation of suzma (strained yogurt/curd) that incorporates beets, giving the dairy product a distinct flavor and striking color.
* **Iradi Plov:** A regional variation distinct from standard Central Asian plov due to its absolute avoidance of horsemeat (utilizing prime grain-finished beef or high-altitude mountain mutton instead, with any *kazy* made of beef or lamb). It uses cold-tolerant, short-grain sticky rice introduced to the lower Chu River Basin by Koryo-saram deportees, is seasoned with Wild Mountain Cumin from the Talas Alatau, and is traditionally served with Beet Suzma to offset the meat's richness.

## Linguistic and Generational Shifts
* **New Lore:** Irad has experienced a profound linguistic and cultural shift since the 1980s.
    * **Urban Boomers & Gen X ("Russos"):** Raised in a Soviet-dominated media landscape, this demographic identifies as "Russos." They remain culturally and linguistically tethered to the Russosphere, maintain Russian customs, and largely hold right-leaning political views.
    * **Urban Millennials:** Raised in Russian-speaking households but forced to learn English post-ISSR. Due to underfunded educational infrastructure and a lack of qualified teachers, their English proficiency carries a distinct, rigid ESL quality.
    * **Zoomers:** The first generation to achieve broad, systemic English fluency. They are also driving a massive cultural resurgence of the Iradi language, mirroring the revival of Gaelic in Ireland.
    * **Tribal Regions:** Rural, tribal-aligned populations actively reject both Russian and English, having continuously maintained Iradi as their exclusive language.

## International Cultural Impact: Iradimania
* **New Lore:** "Iradimania" was a brief but intense period of American cultural fascination with Irad lasting from approximately 1992 to 1994. Following the collapse of the Soviet Union and the establishment of US territorial administration, the American public became captivated by the perceived cultural parallels with Iradi society, romanticizing them as "freedom-loving Christian cowboys" of the Central Asian steppe.
* **The "Swift Hooves" Compilation (1993):** The high-water mark of Iradimania was the 1993 release of *Swift Hooves*, a highly successful compilation album featuring country and folk songs about Irad written and performed by prominent American artists. All profits from the album were directed towards Christian-run charities operating within Irad.

## Geopolitical Development: 2025 Trump Remarks and Backlash
* **New Lore:** In 2025, Donald Trump publicly stated that he was exploring selling Irad to Russia to keep China from acquiring it. It remains unknown whether he realized that Irad had ceased to be an unincorporated U.S. territory decades earlier after the withdrawal from the Alimbekov-Baker Treaty in 2006. The remark provoked intense outrage across the country, triggering widespread demonstrations and fueling acute anti-American sentiment within both the ISEZ and the Autonomous Tribal Regions.

## Cultural Institutions
* **Irad Palace of Art:** The nation's foremost gallery and art preservation foundation. It is headquartered in the city of Iyık Janyl.
* **Irad Film Society:** A non-profit organization dedicated to the preservation and restoration of Iradi films. They organize the biannual Talas International Film Festival. The festival historically features a large slate of Russian-language films. While 2005 is widely considered its most successful year, attendance and participation have fallen off in recent years.
* **Nurzhan Polytechnic Institute:** Formerly known as the Kobozev Polytechnic Institute, it is the nation's foremost technical university, specializing in Petroleum Engineering.

## Major Domestic Corporate Entities
1. **NurGaz (The Nurzhan Oil and Gas Company):** Founded as an ISSR state enterprise in Nurzhan, privatized after 1991 into an oligarchic firm led by Kamran Bekzatov. Controls domestic hydrocarbon extraction, piping crude north toward Kazakhstan and Russia.
2. **Attelkom:** Formed during the US territorial period from a defense telecommunications contract servicing Talas Air Base. Largest domestic ISP and telecom provider in the ISEZ.
3. **IradOne:** Founded in the early 1990s as the state public broadcaster, consolidated under direct DPI editorial control. Programming is primarily in English with some Iradi language content. The company also operates a Russian-language sister channel, **ИрадОдин (IradOdin)**, which is widely considered to have significantly lower production values and worse quality programming compared to the main channel.
4. **Talasbank (Bank of Talas):** Chartered in 1993 following financial reforms and dollarization under the territorial administration.
5. **Sary-Bulak Antimony Works (SBAW):** Heavy metallurgy firm established in 1961 during Soviet industrialization; privatized in 1994, exporting antimony ores to China.
6. **Akyel Equine Logistics & Breeding Syndicate:** Manages pedigree certification and global transport logistics for Chongtor and Akyel horses.
7. **Trans-Chuy Freight & Logistics:** Commercial logistics carrier running bonded trucking routes across the ISEZ and northern border crossings.
8. **Zhetysu Industrial Textiles:** Light-manufacturing plant producing wool, felt, and cotton thread, facing water shortage bottlenecks.
9. **Iradiya (Irad Air Services):** Regional charter and air-cargo operator founded in 1997 operating out of civil hangars adjacent to Talas Air Base.
10. **Nurzhan Agro-Industrial (NAI):** Consolidated agricultural firm specializing in animal feed milling, silage distribution, and bulk grain supply.

## Territorial Footprint & Borders
* **New Lore:** Irad's territory largely overlaps the Talas and Jalal-Abad regions of Kyrgyzstan, together with the northern and eastern rim of the Fergana Valley in Uzbekistan (rural and foothill districts only; the valley's large cities, such as Namangan and Andijan, lie outside Irad) and parts of the Turkistan and Jambyl regions of Kazakhstan.
* **Neighbors and Claimants:** Irad borders only Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, and Kyrgyzstan, and all three claim it *de jure*. Tajikistan neither borders Irad nor claims it. Irad has no border with China or Afghanistan, and remains doubly landlocked.
* **Corrects:** Earlier references to four neighboring claimant states, a Tajik border, and an "Afghan border" are superseded. Tajiks remain a small minority population (about 1% with Uyghurs and Dungans). The antimony sites formerly named after Sary-Tash and Kadamjay (real places in Kyrgyzstan's Osh and Batken regions, outside Irad) are renamed **Sary-Bulak** (mine) and **Chatkal** (smelting and processing), matching their placement in the southern Chatkal and Talas Alatau foothills.

## Real-World Features Within the Footprint
* **Toktogul Reservoir and the Naryn Cascade:** The Soviet-built Toktogul Hydroelectric Station and its reservoir on the Naryn River lie in Irad's Jalal-Abad territory. The station's release schedule balances winter power generation against summer irrigation demand downstream, which makes it a recurring point of friction with Uzbekistan and Kazakhstan.
* **Mailuu-Suu Uranium Legacy:** The valley town of Mailuu-Suu processed uranium ore for the Soviet atomic program from the 1940s into the late 1960s, leaving tailings dumps in landslide-prone terrain. This is the known legacy behind the territorial-era pledge not to develop Irad's uranium and thorium deposits.
* **Arslanbob and Sary-Chelek:** The Arslanbob walnut forests, regarded as among the largest natural walnut forests in the world, and the Sary-Chelek Biosphere Reserve are well-known natural sites in the Chatkal range. Walnuts and wild fruit are a rural export in the tribal regions.
* **Battle of Talas (751):** The clash between Abbasid and Tang forces in the Talas River valley is part of Irad's regional history. Tradition links it to the westward spread of papermaking, though historians dispute that.
* **Fergana Rim:** The northern and eastern Fergana Valley holds the irrigated cotton and orchard belt behind Irad's cotton sector and its water-compliance concerns. It also ties to the Dayuan "heavenly horses" and the Akyel breed's links to the Fergana horse already described in the baselore.

## The Toktogul Complex and the Naryn Water and Power Protocol
* **Split Control:** The Toktogul dam, reservoir, and generating hall lie on the tribal side of the Qara-Chik Demarcation Line, in the Autonomous Tribal Regions. The complex's high-voltage switchyard, the national dispatch centre, the downstream stations of the Naryn cascade, and every export transmission line lie in ISEZ-administered territory. Neither side can release a cubic metre of water or sell a megawatt of power without the other.
* **Output:** Toktogul was built with 1,200 MW of capacity, which makes Irad a net electricity exporter, mainly to Uzbekistan and Kazakhstan, alongside its oil sector.
* **The Protocol:** The 2007 Bishkek Ceasefire Agreement includes the Naryn Water and Power Protocol, which sets the annual release schedule between winter generation and summer irrigation. A joint technical commission of ISEZ and ATR engineers runs it under UN supervision, with observers from Uzbekistan, Kazakhstan, and Kyrgyzstan. This is the main reason all three neighbors sit at the ceasefire table.
* **Public / Journalistic Framing:** Officially, the arrangement is a technical cooperation agreement and revenue is shared under a formula that has never been published. Both the DPI and tribal leaders deny profiting at the other's expense, and independent analysts dispute how the revenue is actually divided. Either side's walkout would risk the ceasefire, which both treat as an unspoken reason to keep the status quo.

## Canon Decisions from the 2026 Wiki Review
These decisions were settled during the full review of the three wiki volumes. They supersede conflicting wording in `baselore.md` and `Irad_Wiki_Original.md` until those files are revised. Full detail and sources are in `CHANGELOG_wiki_review.md`.

* **Timeline clarification:** Toktarov proclaimed the ISEZ from Nurzhan on **25 February 2007**, which began the Second Civil War. The Popular Front stormed Talas on **4 June 2007**, dissolving the Democratic Republic of Irad, and the DPI took the cities that summer. The September 2007 Bishkek Ceasefire froze the line. (This refines the single-sentence wording in baselore's ISEZ entry.)
* **Legal status:** Western Sahara model. Irad has been on the UN list of non-self-governing territories since 1992 and stays listed "by US demand" because Washington never notified the UN that its administration ended in December 2006; the US neither reports on the territory nor seeks its removal. Twenty-six states recognised the Democratic Republic of Irad; a minority keep some recognition of a "Republic of Irad". The Supreme Court's **Statutory Independence Ruling** (Chief Justice Dastan Isakov) is dated **7 December 2006**. Territorial-era residents were **US nationals, not citizens**; that status ended in December 2006.
* **Administrative divisions:** ISEZ = three cities (Talas, Nurzhan, Iyık Janyl) and five districts (Talas, Kara-Buura, Chu, Sary-Bulak, Lower Naryn; Lower Naryn's centre is Tash-Kömür, also the UNMI sector HQ). ATR = 24 clan territories with no formal districts; the UN uses six planning zones (Northern Steppe, Karatau, Talas Alatau, Chatkal Highlands, Toktogul–Naryn, Fergana Rim). The Qara-Chik line is 420 km. Toktogul town, Kara-Köl and Mailuu-Suu lie on the tribal side.
* **Geography composition:** most of Irad's 196,754 km² is steppe and desert rangeland in the north (the lower Chu lowlands, the Moiynkum sands **between the Chu and the Talas**, the southern Betpak-Dala **north of the Chu**, and the Karatau foothills), elsewhere part of Kazakhstan's Jambyl and Turkistan regions. Most people live in the river valleys. The Talas ends in the Moiynkum; the Chu ends near the Akzhaykyn depression on the Northern Steppe.
* **Population arithmetic:** the 2018 city figures cover all territory under each city government; metro and agglomeration figures are estimates outside the census. Only about 125,000 ISEZ residents live outside the three cities, so ISEZ villages are small and district totals are never given. The 1989 Soviet census counted 4,257,000.
* **Legislature and governorship:** territorial era: an interim legislature from October 1992 (bicameral: Senate 15, House 35), first general elections 1994. Since 2007: a single-chamber *Mejlis* of 60 seats (2023: DPI 51, independents 6, Russo-aligned 3); Speaker since 2019 **Nurbek Sultanov** (DPI). The **Elective Governor Acts** are territorial statutes of December 1992: four-year terms, governor and lieutenant governor elected on a joint ticket on the first Tuesday after the first Monday in November, with terms starting on the fourth Monday of November; the two-consecutive-term limit was removed in 2013. Legal scholars differ on whether a formal declaration of incapacity is needed before the Lieutenant Governor exercises the Governor's powers in his own right; none has been made. Maksat was Toktarov's running mate in 2019 and 2023 (Daniyar Tologonov in 2011 and 2015). Territorial governors: Ermatov (1995–2003), Abdrakhmanova (2003–06); US High Commissioners: Ashby, Whitaker, Lindqvist, Garrity.
* **First Civil War:** the siege of Talas ran **10 April – 18 June 1992**, broken by Operation Starlight (began 6 June 1992); about 14,000 dead in the war.
* **2018 border clashes:** with **Uzbek** border guards, June 2018, on the western desert rangelands. The **Northern Border Demarcation Protocol** (2008) is not a treaty: it is UN-facilitated technical minutes between the ISEZ and Kazakh border and customs services, which Kazakhstan says do not affect its claim; patrols are coordinated, not joint.
* **UNESCO:** the Church of St. Janyl and the Ruins of Old Iyık Janyl were inscribed together as one property in **2004** on a US nomination; the Kok-Tash Kariz and Rabat-i-Safed were added in **2014** as Kazakh and Kyrgyz components of the Silk Roads listing. Irad itself is not a State Party. (This corrects the "four sites" framing above.)
* **Church and calendar:** the Feast of St. Janyl is **24 August** (pilgrimage season late August–September). Church tradition places Janyl in the **11th century**; some chronicles associate her with Qara Khitai rule in the 12th. The church at Iyık Janyl was founded in 1727 and consecrated in 1730. Kokand built the Talas citadel fort in the **1820s**. The Cathedral Seminary of Iyık Janyl is formally St. Ephrem Major Seminary. The church uses the Gregorian calendar.
* **Economy:** Irad has **one refinery**, Nurzhan Refinery No. 1 (late 1950s, expanded 1974, 50,000 bpd nameplate). Crude leaves through the 85-km **Northern Export Feeder** into the KazTransOil network near Shymkent (opened November 2000), never through the CPC. The **som** has been fixed at **5 per US dollar** since 1993 (100 tyiyn), issued by the **Bank of Irad** (1993 currency act; reconstituted 2008) under currency-board rules alongside the dollar.
* **Symbols and holidays:** the flag was adopted by decree in May 1992 and confirmed by the State Symbols Act 1993. The anthem *The Spirit of Irad* is by Asan Jumabaev (music) and Cholpon Myrzakulova (words). ISEZ public holidays are set by the Holidays and Commemorative Days Act (1993, revised 2008). The ulak tartysh trophy is the **President's Cup** (instituted 1992 by Alimbekov). The Talas film festival is **biennial** (odd-numbered years), not "biannual".
* **State Archives fond scheme:** 3 Kokand and church charters; 21 Northern Semirechye and Talas districts (1916–36); 85 Central Committee of the Communist Party; 86 Talas City Committee; 108 Revolution of 1992 and Provisional Government; 110 territorial legislative journals; 111 Supreme Court reports; 131 military records; 160 hydrological surveys; 175 ethnography; 204 Iradneft; 236 collective farms; 501 ISEZ executive directives.

## Iyık Janyl Relocated to the Fergana Foothills (Lore Change, October 2026)
* **New Lore:** Iyık Janyl lies at 1,280 m in a side valley in the foothills of the Fergana Range, on the east side of the Naryn gorge about 20 km north-east of Tash-Kömür. It sits inside the ISEZ's southern salient on the Naryn and is surrounded by the Lower Naryn District, while remaining a city of governorate significance administered separately from the district. It is reached from Talas by the Fergana Mountain Transit Highway over the Fergana Gateway and a 12-km branch road above Tash-Kömür, about 235 km in all, so the holy city depends on the same guarded corridor as the Naryn power stations.
* **Climate:** dry-summer continental (Köppen *Dsa*): July highs about 31 °C, January lows about −7 °C, about 560 mm of precipitation, mostly in spring.
* **Setting:** the Qara-Chik line runs along the crests north and east of the city (Toktogul–Naryn zone of the ATR), and the Mailuu-Suu valley of the Fergana Rim lies to the south. The main minorities are Kyrgyz, Uzbeks and Russians. The archeparchy covers the lower Naryn and the Fergana foothills.
* **Corrects:** earlier text placing the city in a side valley on the northern slopes of the Talas Alatau, 65 km south-east of Talas. The legend of Janyl's cave in the Talas Alatau and the Kokand-era history (including incorporation into the Fergana Oblast in 1876) are unchanged; the Fergana-side location fits them.

## Mor-Pop as "Dark K-Pop"; K-Pop in Irad (October 2026)
* **Mor-Pop (revised):** Mor-Pop is Irad's equivalent of "dark K-pop", the darker, concept-driven end of South Korean idol pop: idol groups and soloists, synchronized choreography, high-production music videos and concept "comebacks", set to Mor-Wave's synthesizer sound with Iradi lyrics and English hooks. It **keeps Mor-Wave's usual Turkic pagan imagery** (Morkhan bull masks and horns, the goddess Umay, wolves, golden eagles, shamanic frame drums, dusk-violet staging). Love and city life remain common subjects, but they are framed through that imagery.
* **Corrects:** earlier text saying Mor-Pop's lyrics turn from Mor-Wave's mythological themes to love and city life.
* **K-pop in Irad:** South Korean K-pop is popular in Irad, mainly among urban young people in Talas and Nurzhan, through streaming and fan clubs; the Koryo-saram community's Korean cultural associations are among its early promoters. Mor-Pop producers adopted the K-pop industry model (auditions, trainee groups, comebacks).
* **Unchanged:** Mor-Pop is still a commercial offshoot of the Mor-Wave second wave (mid-2010s), played on IradOne and commercial radio, and criticized within the Mor-Wave scene for commercializing the style. Clergy objections to Morkhan imagery apply to Mor-Pop as well as Mor-Wave.
